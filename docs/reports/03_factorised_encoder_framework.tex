\section{Research principle and starting point}

The support-oracle experiment suggested that the principal loss was not merely energy sparsity. The next aim was to
search for structured codebooks without deciding in advance that one named scheme---ODMA, CCS, SPARC, or another---was
the answer. The correct unconstrained reference is the full common codebook
\[
 \Phi=[\phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{M-1}]\in\C^{n\times M},\qquad M=2^B,
\]
and the full count vector
\[
 a=(a_0,\ldots,a_{M-1})^T\in\Z_+^M,qquad y=\Phi a+z.
\]
Every column is one complete $B$-bit message. At $B=12$, $M=4096$; at $B=14$, $M=16384$; at $B=100$, the explicit
matrix and even the length-$M$ vector are impossible to store. Nevertheless, the explicit model is the right algebraic
starting point because it defines the broad model class before computational restrictions are imposed.

The research philosophy is to remove exponential storage or search with the weakest structural restriction that works,
and to measure each restriction against the broad explicit class. A compact representation is useful only if it retains
good codebook geometry and admits complete-message recovery.

\section{Layer 1: latent contribution decomposition}

Suppose a complete codeword is the sum of $L$ contributions. Component $\ell$ has a local alphabet of $N_\ell$ full-
frame atoms
\[
 \Phi_\ell=[\phi_{\ell,1},\ldots,\phi_{\ell,N_\ell}]
 \in\C^{n\times N_\ell}.
\]
Global message $m$ chooses index $i_\ell(m)\in\{1,\ldots,N_\ell\}$. Define the one-hot selection matrix
\[
 T_\ell\in\{0,1\}^{N_\ell\times M},\qquad T_\ell[:,m]=e_{i_\ell(m)}.
\]
Then the complete waveform and codebook are
\[
 \phi_m=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\phi_{\ell,i_\ell(m)},
 \qquad
 \boxed{\Phi=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\Phi_\ell T_\ell.}
\]
This does not require the message to be semantically split. The fully general one-shot codebook is the special case
\[
 L=1,\qquad \Phi_1=\Phi,\qquad T_1=I_M.
\]

For global counts $a$, define local aggregate counts
\[
 s_\ell=T_\ell a\in\Z_+^{N_\ell}.
\]
Then
\[
 y=\Phi a+z=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\Phi_\ell s_\ell+z.
\]
Each $s_\ell$ is many-to-one: it records how many active messages used each local atom, not which atoms across different
components belonged to the same complete message. This observation later becomes the association problem.

\section{Layer 2: product structure within a component}

Component $\ell$ may itself be generated by resource operators and local codewords. Let
\[
 R_{\ell,q}\in\C^{n\times d_\ell},\quad q=1,\ldots,Q_\ell,
 \qquad
 C_\ell=[c_{\ell,1},\ldots,c_{\ell,V_\ell}]\in\C^{d_\ell\times V_\ell}.
\]
For a fixed operator $q$, $R_{\ell,q}C_\ell$ contains all $V_\ell$ codewords passed through that operator. The complete
product library is
\[
 B_\ell=[R_{\ell,1}C_\ell\mid\cdots\mid R_{\ell,Q_\ell}C_\ell]
 \in\C^{n\times Q_\ell V_\ell}.
\]
Not every pair $(q,v)$ must be legal. Let
\[
 U_\ell\in\{0,1\}^{Q_\ell V_\ell\times N_\ell}
\]
select $N_\ell$ valid local atoms, with one nonzero per column for an exact selection. Thus
\[
 \Phi_\ell=B_\ell U_\ell.
\]
Substitution gives the central framework:
\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi=\sum_{\ell=1}^L
 [R_{\ell,1}C_\ell\mid\cdots\mid R_{\ell,Q_\ell}C_\ell]U_\ell T_\ell.}
\]

The factors have different meanings and should not be conflated:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.22\textwidth}YY@{}}
\toprule
Object and shape & Meaning & Computational or modelling implication \\
\midrule
$L$ & Number of additive contributions & $L>1$ creates a path/association problem unless the tuple is constrained \\
Operator $R_{\ell,q}$\newline $n\times d_\ell$ & Placement, mask, spreading, or transform & Controls resource occupation and geometry \\
Alphabet $C_\ell$\newline $d_\ell\times V_\ell$ & Local value/codeword alphabet & Natural continuous learning target \\
Validity $U_\ell$\newline $Q_\ell V_\ell\times N_\ell$ & Legal local $(q,v)$ pairs & Usually a fixed discrete map \\
Mapping $T_\ell$\newline $N_\ell\times M$ & Complete message to local atom & Contains global semantics and is exponential if stored arbitrarily \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\section{Representation library}

The value of the factorisation is that named constructions become controlled restrictions of one global model.

\subsection{Dense one-shot codebook}

For arbitrary $\Phi_{\rm dense}\in\C^{n\times M}$, take
\[
 L=1,\ Q_1=1,\ d_1=n,\ V_1=N_1=M,
\]
\[
 R_{1,1}=I_n,\quad C_1=\Phi_{\rm dense},\quad U_1=T_1=I_M.
\]
No model-class restriction has been introduced.

\subsection{Sparse-global codebook}

Use the same one-component representation but constrain each column of $C_1$:
\[
 \|C_1[:,m]\|_0=s,qquad \|C_1[:,m]\|_2=1.
\]
Supports may be independent across messages. This preserves a broad family of $\binom ns$ masks and became the most
promising explicit control.

\subsection{ODMA/product construction}

Let each $R_q$ place or mask a shared local codebook $C$. With all pairs legal,
\[
 \Phi=[R_1C\mid\cdots\mid R_QC],\qquad L=1,\quad U=T=I_{QV},\quad M=QV.
\]
ODMA is a sparse placement special case. Sharing only $Q$ supports across $M$ messages is a much stronger restriction
than giving every message its own sparse support.

\subsection{Restricted pattern-codeword pairs}

If only a subset of $(q,v)$ pairs is valid, $U$ selects them:
\[
 \Phi=[R_1C\mid\cdots\mid R_QC]U,qquad N=M,qquad T=I_M.
\]
This separates the geometry of the available product library from the combinatorial selection of messages.

\subsection{Sectioned CCS or SPARC-style construction}

Let $m$ map to one local index $i_\ell(m)$ in each section. Then
\[
 \phi_m=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\Phi_\ell e_{i_\ell(m)}.
\]
Disjoint-section CCS uses $R_{\ell,1}$ to embed a section dictionary into its own channel-use interval. A SPARC-like
representation may instead use $R_{\ell,1}=I_n$ and $C_\ell=\sqrt{E_\ell}A_\ell$. An outer code constrains which tuples
$(i_1,\ldots,i_L)$ are legal; algebraically that constraint is carried by the collection of $T_\ell$ maps.

\subsection{Spreading, interleaving, and hybrid families}

Dense diagonal signatures, permutations, interleavers, slot embeddings, and learned linear maps are all possible
$R_{\ell,q}$. A hybrid may use several components, each with its own operator bank and local alphabet. The factorisation
is a representation of transmitted waveforms; it does not imply that conventional decoders for all these schemes are
the same.

\section{Explicit versus implicit implementation}

The non-scalable mathematical object is the matrix $\Phi\in\C^{n\times M}$. The implementation avoids materialising
it by storing:
\begin{itemize}
 \item the continuous $R$ and $C$ factors or compact operator parameters;
 \item integer arrays that identify each legal local atom, replacing dense one-hot $U$;
 \item a length-$M$ \texttt{msg\_to\_atom} lookup, replacing dense one-hot $T$.
\end{itemize}

For a vector $v\in\C^M$, the forward operator is
\[
 \Phi v=\sum_\ell B_\ell U_\ell(T_\ell v).
\]
$T_\ell v$ aggregates global coefficients by local atom; $U_\ell$ scatters them into the $(q,v)$ product library;
$C_\ell$ forms local vectors; and $R_{\ell,q}$ maps them to the resource grid. The exact adjoint reverses the chain:
\[
 \Phi^Hr=\sum_\ell T_\ell^HU_\ell^HB_\ell^Hr.
\]
This reduces storage from $O(nM)$ to the factor storage plus $O(M)$ integer mappings and decoder state. It is valuable at
$B=12$ or 14, but it is not a $B=100$ solution because both $T_\ell$ and the output $\Phi^Hr\in\C^M$ retain the global
message axis.

\section{Gradient flow through the factorisation}

Let a batch of global counts be $A\in\R^{P\times M}$ and let the encoder output be $Y=A\Phi^T$. For loss $\mathcal L$
with upstream gradient $G_Y=\partial\mathcal L/\partial Y$, the count gradient is the adjoint operation
\[
 \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial A}=G_Y\Phi^*.
\]
For one component and operator, write the aggregated coefficients as $X_{\ell,q}$ and
\[
 Z_{\ell,q}=X_{\ell,q}C_\ell^T,qquad
 Y_\ell=\sum_q Z_{\ell,q}R_{\ell,q}^T.
\]
Then, suppressing conjugation conventions,
\[
 \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial Z_{\ell,q}}=G_YR_{\ell,q}^*,
\]
\[
 \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial C_\ell}
 =\sum_q\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial Z_{\ell,q}}\right)^TX_{\ell,q},
 \qquad
 \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial R_{\ell,q}}
 =G_Y^TZ_{\ell,q}^*.
\]
Autodifferentiation implements these adjoint chains without building $\Phi$. Continuous $C$ and $R$ are learnable.
Exact integer $U$ and $T$ have no ordinary gradient; changing them requires discrete design, search, or an explicitly
labelled relaxation.

\section{Feasibility constraints and learning objective}

The broad factorisation is not a proposal to learn everything simultaneously. The communication problem is
\[
 \min_{\theta,\gamma}\ \mathbb E[\mathcal L(a,D_\gamma(y;\Phi_\theta))]
 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \|\phi_m(\theta)\|_2^2=1\ \forall m,
\]
with final evaluation in PUPE or required $E_b/N_0$, not only surrogate loss.

Important hard conditions are:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item equal unit energy for every global codeword, enforced by parameterisation or projection;
 \item injectivity of $m\mapsto(i_1(m),\ldots,i_L(m))$ unless deliberate ambiguity is being tested;
 \item exact support budgets for sparse families rather than hoping an $\ell_1$ penalty produces them;
 \item balanced resource use and controlled intersections as diagnostics or construction constraints;
 \item fixed $U,T$ in early experiments, so continuous learning cannot silently change message semantics.
\end{enumerate}

The first surrogate combined balanced support BCE with a count Smooth-L1 term. Count MSE,
\[
 \mathcal L_{\rm count}=M^{-1}\|\widehat a-a\|_2^2,
\]
is another simple option, but no differentiable surrogate should be confused with PUPE.

\section{D0: exact data consistency with learned scalar denoising}

For batch size $P$, initialise $a^0=0\in\R^{P\times M}$. Layer $t$ performs
\[
 r^t=y-\Phi a^t,qquad
 g^t=\Re\{\Phi^Hr^t\},qquad
 u^t=a^t+\eta_tg^t.
\]
With known $K_a$, a Bernoulli activity approximation uses $\rho=K_a/M$. D0 converts each coordinate to a support logit
\[
 \lambda_j^t=\operatorname{clip}\left[
 \gamma_t\frac{u_j^t-1/2}{\tau_t}+\beta_t\log\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}+b_t,-30,30\right].
\]
The soft proposal is proportional to $\sigma(\lambda^t)$ and is normalised to total mass $K_a$; damping produces
$a^{t+1}$. Hard nonnegative integer projection occurs only at evaluation.

The physical forward/adjoint pair is exact. Learned scalars calibrate step size, evidence scale, prior, temperature,
bias, and damping. Gradients through all unrolled layers reach both decoder parameters and continuous encoder factors.
D0 still computes an $M$-length vector.

\section{D1: learned nonlocal factor context}

D1 starts with the same $r^t,g^t,u^t$ and base logits. For an $L=1$ product bank, message $j$ has labels $(q_j,v_j)$.
Its features include $u_j^t$, $a_j^t$, $g_j^t$, base logit, pattern mass, residual/noise scales, and prior logit. Latent
slot attention pools context:
\begin{itemize}
 \item among messages sharing the same pattern $q$;
 \item across patterns for a shared local value $v$;
 \item globally across the message set.
\end{itemize}
A small network adds correction $\Delta_j^t$ to the D0 logit. The correction head is zero-initialised, so D1 initially
equals D0 exactly. For fixed slot count, the operation is linear rather than quadratic in $M$, but it remains indexed
by all $M$ messages and is several times slower.

The factor-aware interpretation must be made carefully: D1 also improves dense controls, so a D1 gain alone does not
prove that product structure was exploited. A causal factor benefit requires matched controls or ablations.

\section{Validation before performance}

The implementation checked:
\begin{itemize}
 \item implicit versus materialised forward maps and adjoint inner products;
 \item selected-column construction;
 \item exact representation of legacy ODMA and reduced sectioned CCS constructions;
 \item tiny exact count ML, including message collisions;
 \item normal $B=14,M=16384$ training without constructing an $n\times M$ matrix.
\end{itemize}
These certify implementation of the intended algebra at materialisable $B$. They do not prove paper-faithful AMP state
evolution or large-$B$ complete-message decoding.

\section{Jobs 021--022 and the promising sparse-global result}

The controlled bank fixed $L=1$, $U=T=I$, and $d=n$. It compared dense, shared product, independently sparse-global,
and four-mask ODMA encoders. The four geometries were $(B,n)=(12,128),(12,256),(14,128)$, and $(14,256)$, under common
D0/D1 training and evaluation.

The defensible conclusions are:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item D0 improved only slightly over its matched-filter calibration, about $0.004$ PUPE on average.
 \item D1 improved high-SNR PUPE by about $0.042$ on average, but did so for dense controls too; a uniquely factor-aware
 gain was not isolated. Median runtime was about $5.2$ times D0.
 \item Product sharing was competitive but did not beat dense controls on average; learning $C$ had mixed effect.
 \item Independent 25\%-sparse global supports stayed within $0.016$ PUPE of dense in all four geometries and were
 essentially tied at $n=256$.
 \item Four-mask ODMA was much worse and left roughly 30\% of rows unused. Equal nonzero fraction therefore did not
 imply equal recovery geometry.
\end{enumerate}

\evidence{Measured jobs 021--022. The sparse-global result is an explicit $M=2^B$ existence result under the tested
learned decoders; it is not yet a compact generator or scalable receiver.}

The original staged programme was therefore sensible: dense $\to$ sparse global $\to$ product/ODMA restrictions
$\to$ valid-atom restrictions $\to L>1$ $\to$ mapping design. The next implementation attempted to remove the remaining
$M$ axis by moving to local section states. Report 4 explains why that execution succeeded algebraically but the chosen
outer association and decoder did not preserve the strong $L=1$ landscape.
