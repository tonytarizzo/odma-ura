{\LARGE\bfseries Polar Bayesian decoding: complete decoder options}\par
{\large From exact identities and approximation algebra to full decisions}\par
{\color{muted}19 October 2026 --- Report 10; design and derivation, before implementation}

\textbf{Purpose.} Assemble the receiver options implied by Reports 7 and 9. A useful decoder description
must reach full-message counts from the observation, including discovery, normalization, multiplicity
and a stopping rule. A moment formula or a local update alone does not do that.
We retain one exact reference family and thirteen execution families, with shared variations.
This covers the existing mathematical resources, not every possible future algorithm or combination.

\textbf{Status.} These are mathematical designs, with explicit finite constructions and conditional
guarantees. None has been implemented or ranked in this report. Several remain exponentially expensive.
The new derivations close product-law discovery, auxiliary-field importance correction and a sequential
particle option. Report 7 continues to own exact foundations; Report 9 owns approximation bounds A--T;
Report 8 supplies the frozen encoder. The design log is \path{docs/POLAR_DECODER_DESIGN_LOG.md}.
The identifiers DEC-01--DEC-13 are unrelated to the historical D0--D4 receiver ladder.

\section{The same problem and decision for every option}
\label{sec:contract}
The known encoding rule maps a full message $w=(p,v)$ to
\[
 \phi(p,v)=S_p\diag(\sqrt{e_p})(-1)^{Lv},\qquad
 \|\phi(w)\|=1,\qquad
 y=\sum_{k=1}^K\phi(w_k)+z,\quad z\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_n).
\]
Patterns and payloads are unknown. $P=2^{B_p}$, $V=2^{B_c}$ and $M=PV=2^B$.
The receiver knows $K$, $\sigma^2>0$, and the fixed polar/CRC, placement and balanced-energy construction.
No option changes the physical noise or assumes the active patterns are supplied.

Under the primary IID uniform source, a count vector $a$ and an ordered tuple $W$ have weights
\begin{equation}
 \omega(a)=\frac{K!}{M^K\prod_w a_w!}\,g(y-\Phi a),\qquad
 \omega_{\rm slot}(W)=M^{-K}g\!\left(y-\sum_k\phi(w_k)\right),\qquad
 g(r)=e^{-\|r\|^2/(2\sigma^2)}.
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
Both normalize by the same evidence $Z$. Summing ordered tuples with the same counts gives the factorial
weight in the first expression. A sampling proposal need not be exchangeable; the target still is.
Do not insert a second factorial factor into ordered-slot weights.

For an output $d$ with nonnegative integer counts and $\sum_wd_w=K$, the loss is
\[
 \ell(a,d)=1-\frac1K\sum_w\min(a_w,d_w).
\]
The sufficient decision statistics are count tails $T_{w,j}=\Pr(a_w\geq j\mid y)$, $1\leq j\leq K$.
Choose the largest available next tail $T_{w,d_w+1}$ until $K$ copies have been allocated.
This greedy rule is exact because each word's tails decrease. MAP, a single sampled tuple and top-$K$
posterior means are generally different decisions.

The distinct-message control uses ordered prior $1/(M)_K$ on injective tuples, or uniform prior
$1/\binom MK$ on $0/1$ counts. Require $K\leq M$ and output distinct words there.
It retains collisions of patterns with different payloads. Every extension to it below keeps those
exclusions explicitly; an IID approximation theorem is not automatically a distinct-source theorem.

\subsection{What makes an option a full decoder}
Each option specifies: a finite construction from the known encoder and $y$; how full words are
discovered or enumerated; the legal law or exact queries being evaluated; count-tail readout; a feasible
allocation and stopping rule; and the cost/error that remains. Computational infeasibility is permitted
at this design stage, but uncharged inner or posterior oracles are not.

For later implementations the common interface remains
\[
 \texttt{run(scenario, **params) -> (counts, meta)}.
\]
Here \texttt{counts} may be a sparse full-word map internally; a mandatory dense $M$-vector adapter
would itself cost $M$ and must be counted. The mathematical output contract does not require that adapter.
Relevant metadata are the source, variant, resource budget, realized construction/query work, target-bias
bound, numerical error/failure budget and conditional excess-loss certificate. No implementation is added now.

\section{Shared readout, stopping and error rules}
\label{sec:interfaces}
\subsection{From tails or intervals to a feasible action}
If estimated tails have uniform error $e$ and the action maximizes their gain, Report 9 gives
\begin{equation}
 R(\widehat d\mid y)-R(d^*\mid y)\leq\min(1,2e).
 \label{eq:decision}
\end{equation}
Clip estimates to $[0,1]$ and sort each word's tails in decreasing order if necessary.
Order statistics cannot increase uniform distance from the true sorted tails, so this preserves the
bound and makes greedy allocation valid. It does not assert joint consistency of unrelated word estimates.

For interval-only calculations, let $[l_{w,j},u_{w,j}]$ contain every exact tail.
Choose a feasible incumbent maximizing lower-tail gain, using monotone closures of valid bounds.
For each word these closures are $l_j\leftarrow\max_{r\geq j}l_r$ and
$u_j\leftarrow\min_{r\leq j}u_r$, after intersection with $[0,1]$.
An upper bound on any feasible gain is obtained by maximizing the upper-tail gain under the same budget.
If these bounds are $L_*$ and $U_*$, then
\[
 R(\widehat d\mid y)-R(d^*\mid y)\leq\min\{1,(U_*-L_*)/K\}.
\]
DEC-03 extends this rule to unresolved prefixes. At a resource limit, return the incumbent with the
remaining bound, possibly one. A prefix, latent state or field sample is never itself a recovered word.
Ties may prevent strict optimality certification; an arbitrary positive excess-risk tolerance is a
different stopping requirement.

\subsection{A common independent-replicate guarantee}
\label{sec:replicates}
Several designs produce independent replicates $(Y_s,X_{s,H})$ for bounded count observables $H$, with
\[
 0\leq X_{s,H}\leq Y_s\leq C,\qquad
 \E Y_s=Z_0>0,\quad \E X_{s,H}=Z_0T_H.
\]
$Z_0$ may be the physical evidence, a fixed rescaling, or an explicitly restricted target evidence.
For $J$ queries and failure budget $\delta$, put
\[
 b_S=C\sqrt{\frac{\log[2(J+1)/\delta]}{2S}},\qquad
 \widehat T_H=\frac{\overline X_H}{\overline Y}.
\]
The bounded-variable exponential inequality and a union bound give simultaneous numerator/denominator
errors at most $b_S$. Hence, when $\overline Y>b_S$,
\begin{equation}
 \max_H|\widehat T_H-T_H|
 \leq e_S:=\min\left\{1,\frac{2b_S}{\overline Y-b_S}\right\};
 \quad\text{otherwise use }e_S=1.
 \label{eq:replicate-bound}
\end{equation}
If $\overline Y=0$, do not divide: retain a fixed legal incumbent and the trivial risk bound one,
then continue or stop at the declared budget. For IID sampling the incumbent can be $K$ copies of
one fixed word; for the distinct control it is the first $K$ legal words.
Take $J=MK$ for all word tails, including unseen words, or $J=K(2M-1)$ for all binary message-prefix
count tails. The logarithm is computable from $B,K$ without constructing those arrays.
Weights may all be rescaled together; $C$ must use the same scale. It is a proved global bound, not
the largest observed weight. Report 9 H also gives variance-sensitive sample rates.

A positive weighted sample law gives monotone tails automatically. A sample containing count $c$ of a
word updates its first $c$ tails, so only $K$ updates are needed per complete tuple.
Unseen words have zero empirical tails but remain covered by the simultaneous error bound.
For prescribed rounds, allocate $\delta_r=\delta/[r(r+1)]$. This permits stopping on a certificate.
Freeze a learned-from-computation proposal before using fresh independent draws, or supply a separate
adaptive proof; numerical independence cannot be inferred from an effective-sample-size diagnostic.

\newpage
\subsection{Keep target approximation separate from numerical uncertainty}
If support restriction costs posterior mass $\eta$, a positive conditional target approximation has TV
error $\tau$, and its normalized tails have numerical error $e$, then a valid total error is
\begin{equation}
 e_{\rm total}\leq\eta+(1-\eta)\min(1,\tau+e).
 \label{eq:ledger}
\end{equation}
For compatible positive weight modifications with log-ratio range $\Omega$, one may use
$\tau=\tanh(\Omega/4)$. Exact importance correction removes proposal bias, while retaining sampling
error and concentration cost. Signed computations instead supply numerator/evidence intervals.
Do not count a corrected guide error again as posterior bias, or omit an error in the target weights.
All bounds here are in real arithmetic; certified finite-precision enclosures are a later implementation duty.

\section{REF: exact finite Bayes decoding}
\label{sec:reference}
\textbf{In plain terms.} Give every legal explanation its physical likelihood and source probability,
then choose the output with greatest expected number of correctly recovered copies.
This is the reference against which an approximation's query and decision errors can eventually be measured.

A direct construction visits the $H(M,K)=\binom{M+K-1}{K}$ IID count vectors, accumulates $Z$ and each
occupied word's marked count masses, normalizes and allocates copies. It requires at most $K$ positive
count-tail updates per configuration. Computing each signal directly costs $O(KC_{\rm enc}+nK)$;
enumerating $H(M,K)$ configurations remains the dominant issue. Streaming avoids storing every posterior
state, but an all-word tail table can require $O(MK)$ storage.

Report 7 supplies equivalent execution variants:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Excluded-alphabet residual recursion, with a Gaussian base case.
\item Positive inside/outside convolutions over message-prefix alphabets, retaining multiplicities.
\item Exact projected/frontier schedules and resource finalization.
\item Polar/pattern factor elimination with joint separators and count counters.
\item Full Fourier inversion or the full connected-user expansion.
\item Specialized exact polar pair and triangle contractions for $K=2,3$.
\item Certified partial summation that stops before exhaustive evaluation if the Bayes decision separates.
\end{enumerate}
These are not different optimal decoders statistically. Their cost, reuse and intermediate objects differ.
The low-dimensional parity/projection structure can reduce operations without removing explicit alphabet,
joint-interface or reachable-state growth. Equal-signal classes retain source-weighted word identities:
replacing a class by an arbitrary representative would change the loss.

\section{DEC-01: direct residual-count decoding}
\label{sec:direct}
\textbf{In plain terms.} For each possible word, ask how plausible the observation is if that word appears
zero, one, two, or more times. Explain the remaining signal using the other words.

For $M>1$, let $X^{(-w)}$ be uniform on all words except $w$, and define
\[
 L_h^{(-w)}(r)=\E g\!\left(r-\sum_{i=1}^hX_i^{(-w)}\right).
\]
The exact count numerators are
\begin{equation}
 N_{w,j}=\binom Kj M^{-j}(1-1/M)^{K-j}
              L_{K-j}^{(-w)}(y-j\phi_w),\qquad \sum_{j=0}^KN_{w,j}=Z.
 \label{eq:direct-count}
\end{equation}
Exclusion is essential: leaving $w$ in the remainder produces factorial moments instead.
$M=1$ is deterministic. A finite decoder visits every word, evaluates or bounds these $K+1$ masses,
normalizes per word, takes tails and applies Section~\ref{sec:interfaces}.

\textbf{Retained variations.}
Use F's Gaussian smoothed-residual formula; a mixture over explicit modes; N's tilted closure with
Q's finite inner queries; or Q's positive likelihood-polynomial hierarchy.
The latter converges with increasing degree without requiring Gaussian interference.
Alternatively query M's factorial moments, with an \emph{unrestricted} remainder, and reconstruct
tail intervals using P's finite moment LP. At full order exact moments determine each word's count law.
These changes affect the query provider, not the decoder's full-alphabet traversal.

Gaussian masses are positive and yield monotone per-word tails, though their separate normalizers need
not be one common approximate evidence. Use direct tail bounds, not an invented joint posterior TV.
For signed providers, bound event and complement masses before division. Shared-evidence and moment
constraints can tighten the intervals without assuming independent errors.

\textbf{Cost and stopping.} If $C_L$ includes a residual query's inner moments, partitions and matrices,
direct work is $O(MKC_L+MK)$ plus tail selection and encoder construction, with possible shared work.
Excluded moments use the exact rank-one correction in F. Complete readout is already explicit in $M$.
Choose a query tolerance/degree or return its actual bound at a budget limit. A Gaussian closure alone
need not approach exact inference as numerical precision increases; use the polynomial or exact residual
variant when a convergent refinement is required.
Under distinct sampling, only counts zero and one remain, with the separate excluded distinct residual
and masses from Report 9's completion section.

\section{DEC-02: an optimized product-law decoder}
\label{sec:product}
\textbf{In plain terms.} Approximate the joint posterior by $K$ independent draws from one optimized
message distribution. This is the simplest retained joint law, but it can miss strong dependence.

For $\psi(\theta)=M^{-1}\sum_w e^{\theta^T\phi_w}$ and $\mu=\nabla\log\psi$, S specifies
\[
 \nu_\theta(w)=\frac{e^{\theta^T\phi_w}}{M\psi(\theta)},\qquad
 Q_\theta(W)=\prod_k\nu_\theta(w_k),\qquad
 \sigma^2\theta_*+(K-1)\mu(\theta_*)=y.
\]
Optimize the strictly convex tilt objective with S's finite gradient accuracy bound, or stop at a
declared trial tilt. Q's character polynomials or explicit sums evaluate its partitions/moments.
The true objective value and an evidence upper bound give
\[
 \tau_{\rm var}\leq
 \min\{1,\sqrt{(\log Z_+-F_-)/2}\}.
\]
This certifies the chosen reference only when informative. Exact optimization is not a guarantee of a
good approximation; Report 9 gives a two-user counterexample.

\subsection{Close discovery using the highest-probability words}
Under $Q_\theta$, $a_w\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(K,\nu_\theta(w))$.
Every binomial tail is increasing in its probability, and
$T_{w,j}\leq T_{w,1}$. Let $C$ contain the $\min(K,M)$ highest-probability words.
If an allocation uses a word outside $C$, then some word in $C$ has no allocated copy: there are only
$K$ copies in total. Moving an outside copy to that inside word's first copy does not decrease gain,
since its first tail is at least the removed outside tail. Repeat to obtain an optimal allocation
supported on $C$. Ties can be resolved deterministically. This is a property of this product law,
not a top-$K$ shortcut for the physical interacting posterior.

The probabilities are ordered by $s(w)=\theta^T\phi(w)$.
For a fixed pattern and conditioned payload prefix, Q's character grouping gives
$s(z)=a_0+\sum_{g\ne0}a_g\chi_g(z)$ and upper bound $a_0+\sum_g|a_g|$.
Initialize the $P$ pattern roots, split the highest-bound payload prefix, and evaluate full words at
leaves. Stop when no unresolved bound exceeds the $\min(K,M)$th retained score; include tie-breaking in that
comparison. Full traversal is the finite worst case. Pattern enumeration and key construction are charged.

Evaluate the normalizer and binomial tails on $C$, then allocate $K$ copies by next-tail gain.
With exact partitions this is the exact Bayes action for $Q_\theta$.
Certified partition intervals instead bound each $\nu_\theta(w)$; binomial-tail monotonicity propagates
them. Probability error $\epsilon_p$ gives tail error at most $K\epsilon_p$ by coupling $K$ Bernoulli draws.
Add numerical error to $\tau_{\rm var}$ or use direct allocation intervals.

\textbf{Variations and cost.} The one-user tilt $\theta=y/\sigma^2$ is an unoptimized variant.
One common positive Taylor weight across words gives a coherent polynomial product surrogate; its
additional source-law error must be charged.
Costs include all optimization queries, $P$ roots, visited prefixes, the partition and at most $K$
word-count distributions. The search or partition can still enumerate all $M$ words.
This product law is IID-specific; conditioning it on distinctness requires a separate coupled calculation.

\newpage
\subsection{A complete slot-specific product variation}
The larger family $Q(W)=\prod_k\nu_k(w_k)$ is also finite, but its optimization is generally nonconcave.
Write $\mu_k=\E_{\nu_k}\phi$, and let $u$ be the uniform one-word law. Its evidence objective is
\[
 F(Q)=-\frac{\|y-\sum_k\mu_k\|^2+\sum_k(1-\|\mu_k\|^2)}{2\sigma^2}
              -\sum_k\KL(\nu_k\|u).
\]
Holding the other laws fixed, the exact coordinate optimum is
\[
 \nu_k(w)\propto e^{\theta_k^T\phi_w},\qquad
 \theta_k=\frac{y-\sum_{i\ne k}\mu_i}{\sigma^2}.
\]
Start, for example, from zero tilts and perform a declared number of cyclic coordinate sweeps.
Exact coordinate updates cannot decrease $F$, but give no global optimum guarantee.
With approximate inner moments, treat the resulting tilts as trial references and bound their actual
objective; an optimization improvement is not assumed.

For each word, obtain its count probabilities from
$\prod_k[1-\nu_k(w)+z\nu_k(w)]$ in $O(K^2)$ arithmetic.
Full alphabet traversal, followed by tails and greedy allocation, closes readout; the identical-slot
top-$K$ discovery proof is not reused. Alternatively these product cell-count queries can feed DEC-03,
with the variational error allowance included. The same evidence-gap TV certificate applies by
marginalization, although slot/count KL need not be equal when the factors differ.
The variation costs the coordinate partition/moment work plus $O(MK^2)$ direct readout.
It can represent different likely roles for slots, but remains an independent product and can still miss
essential posterior correlations. Symmetrizing slot labels changes no count law or count decision.

\section{DEC-03: decision-directed message-prefix refinement}
\label{sec:prefix}
\textbf{In plain terms.} Keep large sets of possible words unresolved until their possible contribution
to the output makes them worth splitting. Ask count questions about each set rather than guessing one word.

Maintain a disjoint binary prefix cover $\mathcal C$ of the \emph{message} alphabet. For a cell $D$,
let $A_D=\sum_{w\in D}a_w$ and bound
\[
 G_D(h)=\E\min(A_D,h),\qquad h=0,\ldots,K.
\]
For any allocation of $h$ output copies inside $D$, its matching gain is at most $\min(A_D,h)$
pointwise. If $U_D(h)$ bounds $G_D(h)$, then
\begin{equation}
 U_*=\max_{\substack{h_D\geq0\\\sum_Dh_D=K}}\sum_{D\in\mathcal C}U_D(h_D)
 \label{eq:prefix-upper}
\end{equation}
is an upper bound on the best physical-posterior gain. A budget dynamic program costs
$O(|\mathcal C|K^2)$; concavity may simplify special cases but is not assumed.
Bounds $0\leq G_D(h)\leq h$ always initialize the calculation.

Resolve one legal word initially and assign its $K$ copies for an IID incumbent; for distinct sampling
resolve $K$ distinct words. Retain lower tail bounds on resolved leaves to get incumbent gain $L_*$.
Refine a cell used by an optimistic allocation in \eqref{eq:prefix-upper}, or tighten a query whose
uncertainty controls the gap. Stop when $(U_*-L_*)/K$ is small enough, or return the actual remaining bound.
Resolve full words before output; an unresolved cell is only an upper bound.

\textbf{Query variants.} Use conditional polar moments and excluded-cell residuals; tilted or polynomial
residual queries; marked connected, Fourier or signed-contraction queries; field count polynomials;
or simultaneously bounded configuration samples. P's moment LP, shared evidence and prefix split tables
combine available information. They cannot remove the proven ambiguity of insufficient moments.
Gaussian-only queries can stall. A declared convergent provider or exact subtree summation closes
the finite refinement route. A deterministic fair schedule can interleave promising refinements with
the oldest unresolved cell/query; budget termination does not require full refinement.

\textbf{Cost.} Charge every visited prefix and every provider invocation, its preprocessing, the current
cover program and full-word readout. There can be $2M-1$ message-prefix nodes. If a randomized provider
is used adaptively, cover all possible prefix queries or allocate conditional failure budgets to fresh
queries. Choosing favourable estimates after seeing them does not preserve a fixed-query guarantee.

\section{DEC-04: bounded configuration search and list decoding}
\label{sec:configuration}
\textbf{In plain terms.} Build complete explanations of all users. Keep exact weights for explanations
already completed and a rigorous upper bound on everything still unexplored.
This differs from DEC-03: its prefixes belong to the $KB$-bit \emph{ordered tuple}, not one message.

Partition the tuple space into evaluated leaves $\mathcal L$ and a disjoint unresolved prefix frontier
$\mathcal F$. A node $\xi$ has prior mass $\pi(\xi)$ and conditional likelihood
$L(\xi)=\E_\pi[g(y-s_W)\mid\xi]$. Any valid $u_\xi\geq L(\xi)$ gives
\[
 Z_{\mathcal L}=\sum_{W\in\mathcal L}\omega_{\rm slot}(W),\qquad
 U_{\mathcal F}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal F}\pi(\xi)u_\xi,\qquad
 \eta\leq\frac{U_{\mathcal F}}{Z_{\mathcal L}+U_{\mathcal F}}.
\]
The bound follows from the unknown omitted evidence being at most $U_{\mathcal F}$.
Use $u_\xi=1$ at worst, or Report 7's convex-completion/support bounds and Report 9's likelihood
intervals. Those are bounds on unexplored completions, not substitutes for their total prior mass.

Initialize by completing one legal tuple and retaining the sibling prefixes along its path.
This supplies positive $Z_{\mathcal L}$ and a cover of every other tuple.
Expand a frontier node, replace it by its legal children and update the bounds. Complete leaves use
the physical likelihood exactly. Their normalized positive weighted counts give a feasible Bayes action
on the retained list, with excess risk at most $2\eta$, capped at one. Marked adverse-event bounds can
give a sharper decision certificate. Multiple orderings of one count vector must all retain their mass.

\textbf{Variations.} Best-first mass-bound expansion, depth-first traversal and a bounded beam/list
change exploration order or memory. A discarded beam node still belongs to the omitted frontier:
keep its bound, or a valid aggregate bound, instead of silently declaring it impossible.
A message-list restriction is another event and needs a bound for configurations containing omitted words.
Report 7's count-tree version avoids ordered duplicates using its exact factorial prior.

\textbf{Cost and limit.} In the worst case the ordered search has $M^K$ leaves.
Each bound/leaf evaluation and frontier record is charged. The $K$ sparse updates per completed tuple
avoid an additional full alphabet readout. Exhaustion gives REF; a limited list gives an approximate
decision with its actual unresolved bound. Keeping only the best physical residual and returning it
would instead be a MAP-like decision and is not this decoder.

\section{DEC-05: independent guided importance decoding}
\label{sec:importance}
\textbf{In plain terms.} Let a cheap approximation suggest complete tuples, then correct each suggestion
using its exact physical likelihood and the probability with which it was proposed.

At an ordered-tuple bit prefix $\xi$, choose a positive continuation guide $\widetilde L$ and use
\[
 q_g(b\mid\xi)=\frac{\pi(b\mid\xi)\widetilde L(\xi b)}
                    {\sum_c\pi(c\mid\xi)\widetilde L(\xi c)},\qquad
 q_g(W)=\prod_{t=1}^{KB}q_g(b_t\mid\xi_{t-1}).
\]
The prior branch is $1/2$ for IID uniform bits. Two child queries per bit suffice; both pattern and
payload bits are generated. F's conditional Gaussian guide is one starting choice; N/Q, positive
contractions or exact small-block sums give alternatives.
If certified child likelihoods imply posterior branch $p_0\in[l,u]$, the robust guide
$q_0=u/(1+u-l)$ has worst branch ratio $1+u-l$. Its complete ratio bound must cover unvisited prefixes too.

For universal full support use the \emph{whole-tuple} mixture
\[
 q(W)=\epsilon\pi_{\rm slot}(W)+(1-\epsilon)q_g(W),\qquad 0<\epsilon\leq1.
\]
Draw its component, generate a tuple, and evaluate the actual mixture density, including both terms.
Compute
\[
 Y(W)=\omega_{\rm slot}(W)/q(W)\leq1/\epsilon,\qquad
 X_H(W)=Y(W)H(W).
\]
Independent tuples satisfy Section~\ref{sec:replicates}. Accumulate sparse weighted count tails, allocate
copies and stop at a declared sample round/certificate or budget. Guide bias has been corrected.
The self-normalized estimates are not exactly unbiased at finite sample count.

\textbf{Variations.} Prior-only sampling is $\epsilon=1$.
An evaluable product, quantized-state or positive-contraction law can replace the bit guide.
A positive surrogate with log-weight range error $\Omega$ supplies concentration bound
$\sup P/q\leq e^\Omega$ without a prior mixture when it already has full support.
An exact conditional block may be integrated before sampling the rest; Report 9 H proves the centered
unnormalized query variance cannot increase. This may require dense marked queries, so retain an explicit
discovery/readout method when conditional integration removes the sampled words.

\textbf{Cost and limits.} Let $C_q$ include drawing and evaluating the full mixture, including any guide
construction. Direct cost for $S$ tuples is
\[
 O\!\left(S[C_q+KC_{\rm enc}+nK]+SK\log(2+SK)\right),
\]
with $O(SK)$ sparse tail storage. No explicit $M$-array is required by this execution path, but $C_q$
may include pattern sums and the required sample count can be enormous. A good empirical effective
sample size is a diagnostic, not a global unseen-word certificate.
Distinct sampling uses the correct legal prior/proposal and its actual exclusions.

\section{DEC-06: corrected block moves with global refreshes}
\label{sec:chain}
\textbf{In plain terms.} Maintain one complete explanation, revise small groups while retaining all
outside users in the residual, and occasionally propose a completely new explanation.

Use DEC-05's full-support proposal in an independence Metropolis step:
\[
 \alpha(W,W')=\min\left\{1,
 \frac{\omega_{\rm slot}(W')q(W)}{\omega_{\rm slot}(W)q(W')}\right\}.
\]
With fixed probability $\beta>0$ choose this global kernel. Otherwise update a fixed-size slot block
$\mathcal B$ conditional on the current outside messages using
$r_{\mathcal B}=y-\sum_{i\notin\mathcal B}\phi(w_i)$.
Exact one-user polar sums or exact K2/K3 joint contractions supply conditional draws.
Alternatively a guided block proposal uses its exact conditional Metropolis ratio.
Outside messages are current state variables, never supplied truth.

For K2, draw the first word from its exact marginal and the second from its conditional law.
For K3, draw one from its exact slot marginal, then a conditional pair. Independently sampling block
marginals would lose their dependence. Distinct updates exclude outside words and within-block repeats.
Freeze proposal and state-independent block-selection probabilities during a run; otherwise include the
correct selection ratios or derive a separate adaptation argument.

Every kernel preserves the exact target. For a certified $\bar\gamma\geq\sup_W P_{\rm slot}(W\mid y)/q(W)$,
Report 9 L proves a target minorization $\kappa=\beta/\bar\gamma$.
Record $S$ states separated by
\[
 t\geq\kappa^{-1}\log(S/\delta_{\rm mix})
\]
transitions, including before the first record. A coupling bounds their distance from $S$ independent
posterior draws by $\delta_{\rm mix}$, giving simultaneous tail error
\[
 e=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2MK/\delta_{\rm stat})}{2S}}
\]
with total failure at most $\delta_{\rm mix}+\delta_{\rm stat}$, capped as necessary.
Sparse count frequencies and greedy allocation complete the decoder.
An evidence lower bound provides $\bar\gamma\leq W_{\max}/Z_-$.
Even $Z_-=e^{-(\|y\|+K)^2/(2\sigma^2)}$ is valid, but usually much too conservative.

\textbf{Cost and limits.} Charge $St$ transitions, each local contraction and each global proposal,
not just recorded states. Exact K3 still pays repeated pair-operator and alphabet costs.
Local-only chains with an unspecified burn-in have an exact invariant law but lack this finite guarantee.
The proof does not show that block moves outperform independent sampling.
Their role is to explore dependence inside a complete joint decoder, not to declare higher-user effects absent.

\section{DEC-07: sequential particles with a complete count guarantee}
\label{sec:particles}
\textbf{In plain terms.} Preserve several partial explanations, repeatedly allocate more particles to
promising ones, and correct the accumulated guidance at the end. Resampling can help exploration,
but it creates dependence; the certificate below counts independent batches rather than descendants.
Sequential importance/resampling is classical machinery \cite{delmoral2006}; the finite count-query
construction and proof here specify its role in this problem.

\subsection{Prefix-growth variant}
Let $T=KB$, and let $\xi_t$ be the first $t$ tuple bits. Choose fixed positive guide functions
$\ell_t(\xi_t)$ before running a batch, with
\[
 \ell_0=1,\qquad \ell_T(W)=g(y-s_W),\qquad
 \gamma_t(\xi_t)=\pi(\xi_t)\ell_t(\xi_t).
\]
For $0<t<T$, the guides may be clipped Gaussian, tilted or polynomial continuation estimates.
They are intermediate computational weights, not additional physical observations.
Use an evaluable legal branch proposal $q_t(b\mid\xi)$, and incremental weight
\begin{equation}
 G_t(\xi b)=
 \frac{\pi(b\mid\xi)\ell_t(\xi b)}
      {q_t(b\mid\xi)\ell_{t-1}(\xi)}.
 \label{eq:particle-increment}
\end{equation}
Without resampling the product telescopes to the exact tuple importance weight.

One batch contains $N$ particles:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Start with $N$ empty prefixes of equal weight and $\widehat Z_0=1$.
\item At each step, independently choose each parent from the previous normalized weights, then draw
      its next bit from $q_t$. This is multinomial resampling followed by propagation.
\item Compute $G_t^{(i)}$, their average $\overline G_t$, normalized weights
      $w_t^{(i)}=G_t^{(i)}/(N\overline G_t)$, and
      $\widehat Z_t=\widehat Z_{t-1}\overline G_t$.
\item At $t=T$, retain the weighted complete tuples without an additional resampling step.
      Set $Y=\widehat Z_T$ and $X_H=Y\sum_iw_T^{(i)}H(W^{(i)})$.
\end{enumerate}
All weights are positive. Whole tuple histories, or parent pointers sufficient to reconstruct them,
must survive resampling so full-message counts remain available.

\subsection{Why independent batches give valid finite confidence}
Define $\widehat\gamma_t(f)=\widehat Z_t\sum_iw_t^{(i)}f(\xi_t^{(i)})$.
Conditioning on the previous cloud and averaging the multinomial parents and proposed children gives
\[
 \E[\widehat\gamma_t(f)\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
 =\widehat\gamma_{t-1}\!\left(
       \sum_b\pi(b\mid\xi)\frac{\ell_t(\xi b)}{\ell_{t-1}(\xi)}f(\xi b)\right).
\]
Induction from the empty prefix proves
\[
 \E\widehat\gamma_t(f)=\sum_{\xi_t}\gamma_t(\xi_t)f(\xi_t).
\]
In particular $\E Y=Z$ and $\E X_H=ZT_H$. This proves unbiasedness of the unnormalized quantities,
not of each batch's self-normalized tails.

To obtain a deterministic finite bound, ensure
$q_t(b\mid\xi)\geq\epsilon_t\pi(b\mid\xi)$ and
$a_t\leq\ell_t\leq b_t$, with $a_0=b_0=1$ and $b_T=1$.
Positive clipping supplies the intermediate $a_t,b_t$ without asserting their accuracy.
Then
\begin{equation}
 0\leq X_H\leq Y\leq C_{\rm part}:=
       \prod_{t=1}^T\frac{b_t}{\epsilon_t a_{t-1}}.
 \label{eq:particle-bound}
\end{equation}
Run $S$ independent batches and pool their \emph{unnormalized} $X_H,Y$.
Section~\ref{sec:replicates} applies with $C=C_{\rm part}$ and $J=MK$.
The pooled positive law weights terminal particles by $Y_s w_{T,s}^{(i)}$.
It yields sparse count tails and a feasible greedy action. Equal-weight averaging of batch ratios
would not have the displayed unbiased numerator identity.

The bound can be exponentially poor in $KB$; that is explicit, not hidden as a claimed effective sample size.
For fixed $N$, arbitrarily many independent batches still give consistent queries with a finite
confidence prescription. With $N=1$, the construction reduces to independent sequential importance sampling.
This proof does not claim a useful rate for one large correlated cloud.

\subsection{A second variant: temper complete tuples}
Instead of increasing prefix length, use complete tuples and a fixed schedule
$0=\beta_0<\beta_1<\cdots<\beta_T=1$:
\[
 \gamma_t(W)=\pi_{\rm slot}(W)g(y-s_W)^{\beta_t},\qquad
 G_t(W)=g(y-s_W)^{\beta_t-\beta_{t-1}}\in(0,1].
\]
Initialize particles from the true source. At each stage weight by $G_t$, record the mean weight in
$\widehat Z_t$, resample, and optionally apply any kernel invariant for the normalized $\gamma_t$.
DEC-06 supplies such conditional/global Metropolis kernels with the stage likelihood.
The intermediate exponent changes a computational target only; the final physical noise remains $\sigma^2$.

For a marked function $f$, the same induction now uses
$\gamma_{t-1}(G_t K_t f)=\gamma_t(K_t f)=\gamma_t(f)$, by invariance of $K_t$.
At the final stage use the post-resampling/mutation equal-weight empirical measure with $\widehat Z_T$,
or its equivalent pre-resampling weighted measure if no final mutation is made.
Thus independent batches again have unbiased evidence/numerator pairs and now $Y\leq1$.
No equilibrium assumption for each finite mutation run is needed for this identity.
Poor exploration still appears as high relative error when $Z$ is small.
An adaptive temperature schedule selected from the same particles is outside this fixed-schedule proof.

\textbf{Costs.} Prefix growth needs $SNT$ branch/guide operations, propagation and resampling,
plus waveform construction and terminal count reconstruction. Parent pointers require $O(NT)$ records
per active batch; cached signals can add $O(Nn)$ storage and $O(n)$ copying work per propagation.
Pooled sparse tails require at most $SNK$ updates/storage entries before aggregation.
Tempering charges all complete-tuple mutations and stage likelihoods instead.
No claim equates $SN$ correlated terminal particles with $SN$ independent posterior samples.

 \widetilde\omega(a)\propto

 C_{m,D}(z)=\E_{\nu^{\otimes m}}\!

not be replaced by the weight of one chosen auxiliary graph.

\section{DEC-13: positive-field decoding and exact joint correction}
\label{sec:fields}
\textbf{In plain terms.} Replace the joint interaction by shared Gaussian auxiliary variables.
Conditioned on them, the uncorrected retained-field law separates into one-user categorical laws.
Either integrate their count distributions or sample complete tuples together with the fields.

Stack the waveforms as $x_W$ and put $s_W=\sum_k\phi(w_k)$,
$t=y/\sigma^2$, $C=(KI-\one\one^T)\otimes I_n/\sigma^2$.
Choose a retained covariance $BB^T\preceq C$, whitened field $U\in\mathbb R^d$, and define
\[
 D(W)=x_W^T(C-BB^T)x_W,\qquad
 c_y=e^{-(\|y\|^2+K^2)/(2\sigma^2)},\qquad
 f_k(u,w)=e^{(t+(Bu)_k)^T\phi_w}.
\]
The full field has $BB^T=C$ and $D=0$.
For a common retained physical projection, R expresses $D$ as omitted pairwise dispersion and bounds it.

\subsection{Conditional-count integration}
Ignoring $D$ gives tuple weight $\omega_{\rm slot}(W)e^{-D(W)/2}$.
With exact inner sums $\psi_k(u)=M^{-1}\sum_w f_k(u,w)$, its field density is proportional to
$\varphi_d(u)h(u)$, $h=\prod_k\psi_k$, and
$p_{k,w}=f_k/(M\psi_k)$.
Conditional count probabilities for word or cell $A$ are coefficients of
\[
 \prod_{k=1}^K[1-p_{k,A}(u)+z\,p_{k,A}(u)].
\]
Use a bounded importance proposal from R, calculate its field weight, and integrate these bounded
conditional tails. Section~\ref{sec:replicates} applies to independent field samples.
Enumerate full words for readout, or feed cell queries to DEC-03.
The bounded grid construction below gives a deterministic alternative to the field samples.

Direction removal costs at most $\tanh((D_{\max}-D_{\min})/8)$ in TV.
Restricting $\|U\|\leq R$ adds the corresponding posterior mass bound.
Uniform coherent polynomial inner weights add the joint log-ratio bound from Report 9 R.
Propagate these in their actual conditional order. Independent normalization of unrelated inner
approximations does not necessarily define this joint law.

\subsection{A deterministic field integral with an explicit remainder}
For the uncorrected retained-field target and a word/cell count-tail indicator $H$, write
\[
 F_H(u)=\varphi_d(u)M^{-K}\sum_W
              e^{t^Ts_W+(Bu)^Tx_W}H(W),\qquad 0\leq H\leq1.
\]
It is evaluable through the inner partitions and conditional count polynomial; $F_1$ is the evidence
integrand, without $c_y$. Let $a_*=K/\sigma$ and restrict to the cube $[-R,R]^d$.
Each component has logarithmic gradient $-u+B^Tx_W$, with $\|B^Tx_W\|\leq a_*$.
Since all its summands are nonnegative,
\[
 F_H(u)\leq F_{\max}:=(2\pi)^{-d/2}e^{K\|t\|+a_*\sqrt d R},\qquad
 \|\nabla F_H(u)\|\leq(\sqrt d R+a_*)F_{\max}=:\Lambda.
\]
These are uniform over all count marks in $[0,1]$; differentiating an approximate normalized
conditional tail is unnecessary.
A midpoint grid with cell side $\Delta=2R/m$ therefore has integral error at most
\[
 (2R)^d\Lambda\,\frac{\sqrt d\,\Delta}{2}.
\]
The proof bounds the integrand's change from each midpoint by its Lipschitz constant times the
cell radius, then sums cell volumes. Node-value intervals add their volume-weighted errors.
Multiply by $c_y$ for physical unnormalized weights and normalize with a positive denominator bound.

The field posterior is a mixture of unit-covariance Gaussians with centers of norm at most $a_*$.
Leaving the cube implies Euclidean norm above $R$, so its omitted mass is at most
$\Pr(\|Z_d\|>R-a_*)$ for $R>a_*$. This also bounds the change in count law after restriction.
Increasing $R$ and refining the grid gives a finite controlled approximation; it costs $m^d$ nodes
and all their inner/count queries. This is a complete quadrature variation, not an efficient high-dimensional
integration claim. At $d=0$ there is no integral.

\subsection{Keep the auxiliary variable to make exact correction evaluable}
\label{sec:field-joint}
There is a useful complete alternative to evaluating the field-integrated proposal probability of a tuple.
Define an unnormalized target on the \emph{joint} space:
\begin{equation}
 \Omega(u,W)=c_y\varphi_d(u)M^{-K}
       \exp\!\left(t^Ts_W+(Bu)^Tx_W+\frac{D(W)}2\right).
 \label{eq:field-joint}
\end{equation}
Integrating $u$ multiplies by $\exp(x_W^TBB^Tx_W/2)$.
Adding $D/2$ restores $x_W^TCx_W/2=(K^2-\|s_W\|^2)/(2\sigma^2)$, so
$\int\Omega(u,W)\,du=\omega_{\rm slot}(W)$ exactly.
This algebra is the reason a reduced field can guide an exact-target sampler.

Choose an evaluable field proposal $q_U(u)$ and independent conditional categorical guides
$q_k(w\mid u)>0$. Draw $u$, then all $K$ full words, and use
\[
 Y(u,W)=\frac{\Omega(u,W)}
                {q_U(u)\prod_kq_k(w_k\mid u)}.
\]
No marginal $q(W)=\int q_U\prod q_k$ is needed. This is ordinary importance weighting on an
explicit extended space, not an assumption that this marginal density is cheap.

In particular use Q's positive inner polynomials
$\rho f_k\leq\widetilde f_k\leq f_k$, with
$\widetilde\psi_k=M^{-1}\sum_w\widetilde f_k$ and
$q_k=\widetilde f_k/(M\widetilde\psi_k)$.
Draw each word by exact conditional prefix sums of that \emph{same} polynomial.
Then the computable joint weight is
\begin{equation}
 Y=c_y\frac{\varphi_d(u)}{q_U(u)}e^{D(W)/2}
        \prod_k\left[\widetilde\psi_k(u)
                     \frac{f_k(u,w_k)}{\widetilde f_k(u,w_k)}\right].
 \label{eq:field-corrected-weight}
\end{equation}
Both direction removal and inner guide error are corrected. Their price moves into weight
concentration and construction cost. The physical likelihood is still represented exactly once.

\subsection{A finite weight bound and full-word readout}
Let $\overline\Sigma$ be a proved global tilted covariance envelope,
$A=B^T(I_K\otimes\overline\Sigma)B\prec I$, and
$D_A=\det(I-A)^{-1/2}$.
R supplies $q_U=\mathcal N(0,(I-A)^{-1})$ and
$\varphi_d h/q_U\leq D_A h_0$, $h_0=\psi(t)^K$.
For $0\leq D\leq D_{\max}$, \eqref{eq:field-corrected-weight} therefore obeys
\begin{equation}
 Y\leq c_yD_Ah_0 e^{D_{\max}/2}\rho^{-K}.
 \label{eq:field-joint-bound}
\end{equation}
Use a certified upper bound for $h_0$ when computing the numerical bound.
Since the full evidence is at least $c_yh_0$, the exact concentration ratio is at most
$D_Ae^{D_{\max}/2}\rho^{-K}$. The sufficient curvature regime, determinant, dispersion and inner degree
can each make this unattractive. On an unbounded field domain a rule choosing degree from $\|u\|$
must enforce the same $\rho$ for \emph{every} $u$, not just verify sampled points.

Outside this Gaussian-envelope regime, restrict $u$ to a ball and propose
$q_U=\varphi_d\ind_{\|u\|\leq R}/p_R$, where $p_R=\Pr(\|Z_d\|\leq R)$.
For slot-sum-zero retained fields,
$h(u)\leq\exp(K\|t\|+KR/\sigma)$ on the ball. This gives the finite bound
\[
 Y\leq c_y p_R e^{D_{\max}/2}\rho^{-K}
                \exp(K\|t\|+KR/\sigma).
\]
After the $D$ correction, the conditional field given $W$ is still
$\mathcal N(B^Tx_W,I)$, with $\|B^Tx_W\|\leq K/\sigma$.
Thus its exact joint posterior mass outside the ball is at most
$\eta_R=\Pr(\|Z_d\|>R-K/\sigma)$ for $R>K/\sigma$.
The sampler targets this restricted joint law; add $\eta_R$ through the restriction rule.
Rejection sampling the base Gaussian until it lies in the ball costs an expected $1/p_R$ attempts.

Independent joint draws now give sparse full-word tails via Section~\ref{sec:replicates}, followed by
greedy allocation. This closes discovery without a dense table of conditional count polynomials.
The factor $e^{D/2}$ generally recouples words given $u$, so one cannot simultaneously apply the
uncorrected Poisson-binomial readout to those corrected weights.
At full rank with exact inner laws, conditional integration instead gives the earlier Rao--Blackwell
query variation. At zero retained rank the joint sampler becomes a corrected one-user product proposal.

\textbf{Costs and source boundary.} Charge field matrices, sampling/rejection, $K$ inner partition and
prefix-sampling calculations, every pattern sum, exact waveform/dispersion evaluation and sparse readout.
Projection may reduce field dimension while worsening correction concentration.
The IID conditional product is essential to these inner formulas. For distinct tuples the identity
for a fixed $W$ remains true, but one must use the injective prior, legal proposal and its coupled
normalization/bounds. Reusing the IID product integral or the displayed concentration proof is not valid.

\section{Combine the designs without hiding a missing operation}
\label{sec:combinations}
\subsection{Useful compositions with explicit interfaces}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Queries plus selective discovery.}
      DEC-01, 10, 11, 12 and conditional-field DEC-13 can answer DEC-03's cell-count questions.
      Their all-word version is already a finite decoder; the prefix wrapper is a possible reduction in
      query count, not a guarantee that their preprocessing becomes sub-exhaustive.
\item \textbf{Approximate law plus exact correction.}
      DEC-02, 08 and fully normalized positive DEC-09 can propose for DEC-05.
      Backward sampling, density evaluation and normalizers are required operations.
      DEC-13's extended-space construction is a separate way to avoid one intractable marginal density.
      A per-word residual estimate is not automatically a drawable joint law.
\item \textbf{Exact local algebra inside global exploration.}
      Polar K2/K3 contractions supply DEC-06 blocks and DEC-07 tempering mutations or exact conditional
      integration. All outside-user dependence survives in the current residual or exact outer sum.
      No assertion that interactions beyond three users are negligible is needed.
\item \textbf{Reference choice before interaction approximation.}
      DEC-02's center can initialize DEC-10 or a guide, while exact parity grouping precedes DEC-09/12
      features and exact future projection precedes DEC-08 quantization.
      Recentring leaves the field dispersion invariant, so its benefit does not transfer automatically.
\item \textbf{Moment information plus multiple bounded providers.}
      P's count LP and prefix consistency can intersect valid moments/numerators from different
      providers using one common exact evidence. Their approximate point estimates need not form one
      joint law. The low-order obstruction tells when a new query is required.
\item \textbf{Lists and search plus omitted-mass control.}
      Product search or sampling can suggest candidate words; DEC-04 or A's envelope can bound what was
      omitted. Restricting an inference engine to that list costs posterior support mass.
      Alternatively keep full support with a defensive proposal and correct it.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mixtures, query selection and adaptation}
Several full-support proposals $q_r$ can be combined as
$q=\sum_r\lambda_rq_r$ with fixed nonnegative weights summing to one.
Draw a component but weight using the \emph{mixture} density, which requires every contributing $q_r(W)$.
If each $P/q_r\leq\gamma_r$, then
\[
 q(W)\geq P(W)\sum_r\lambda_r/\gamma_r,\qquad
 \sup_W P(W)/q(W)\leq\left(\sum_r\lambda_r/\gamma_r\right)^{-1}.
\]
This is a conditional algebraic bound, not a promise that computing all densities is worthwhile.
Incompatible latent spaces cannot be mixed by omitting their marginalization or defining no common target.

Valid deterministic intervals can be intersected. Random intervals need simultaneous coverage across
queries, provider choices and stopping rounds. Use conditional fresh-batch guarantees after adapting a
guide; the report does not certify arbitrary reuse of adaptive samples.
For a final action, use the strongest valid total-tail or direct-gain certificate actually obtained.
Do not add two estimates of the same bias, or declare a bias removed merely because a correction was proposed.

\subsection{Exact ambiguity and incomplete algorithm names}
Restoring source-prior conditionals inside known disjoint equal-signal classes cannot increase TV
(Report 7). Charge the class construction and full-word marks. Selecting a preferred word inside an
ambiguous class does not remove the physical model's nonidentifiability.
Report 11's affine translation update is a concrete alphabet-free local option for DEC-06-style chains:
its IID ordered-slot move preserves the exact posterior and has explicit count-tail queries. It is not
a new complete decoder or a convergence certificate. Report 9 separately bounds near-alias conditional
merging; encoder changes and their receiver-compatibility requirements belong to Report 8.

Generic loopy BP, EP, AMP/VAMP, SC/SCL and a neural guide are not additional derived full decoders here.
They still need count readout, unseen-word handling and error accounting. A message calculation may
guide DEC-05/07; a beam with its correct frontier is a DEC-04 variation. Learning is unnecessary here.
Historical D2 remains an empirical comparator, with no new count-posterior or AMP guarantee from this report.

\section{What is now ready to review}
\label{sec:review}
The result is a list of complete mathematical decoder options, not a commitment to thirteen independent
implementations. A small implementation may share one source/encoder interface, count allocator,
interval/replicate readout and a few query primitives across many variations.
The important distinctions are what is constructed, what is sampled or summed, where approximation
enters, how full messages appear, and what certificate is available.

The additional closure work is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Identical-slot product discovery needs only its highest $\min(K,M)$ word probabilities, with a proved
      exchange argument and finite conditional-character prefix search. A slot-specific product variation
      instead has explicit coordinate updates and Poisson-binomial readout, without the same discovery shortcut.
\item Field sampling can retain its auxiliary variable and use a directly evaluable joint weight.
      This corrects both discarded directions and polynomial categorical guides without evaluating
      an unavailable integrated tuple proposal. Bounded-domain and curvature-regime guarantees remain explicit.
\item Prefix or tempered particle batches have unbiased unnormalized count queries and finite
      independent-batch confidence. Correlated particles are not treated as independent observations,
      and useful concentration remains unproved.
\item Uniform cutset-sector sampling and bounded field midpoint quadrature have explicit proposals or
      integration remainders, so these named variations also have finite complete constructions.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Completion boundary.} Every retained option can return legal full-word counts at a finite
resource budget and state its residual error bound, even if that bound is one. The options with
convergent refinement are identified; standalone Gaussian and variational product approximations have
irreducible modeling-error conditions rather than an automatic exact limit.
No option is claimed tractable at the intended load solely because its inner equations are finite.

\textbf{Review before code.} Decide which execution families and variations are worth retaining for the
first comparison. For each chosen design, fix its query/proposal provider, exact versus approximate
corrections, discovery rule and numerical stopping budget. The subsequent implementation stage should
measure actual tail/decision error, certificate tightness or failure probability, and total construction,
inference and readout cost separately. This report supplies that choice set; it does not select a winner
or start simulations. New derivations should address a specific remaining computational obstacle exposed
by a chosen design.
