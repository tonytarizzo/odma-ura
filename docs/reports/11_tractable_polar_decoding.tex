{\LARGE\bfseries Polar Bayesian decoding: tractable approximations}\par
{\large From effective finite-alphabet receivers to feasible computation}\par
{\color{muted}29 October 2026 --- Report 11; continuing algebraic investigation}

\textbf{Research aim.} The approximations developed so far improve decoding performance while limiting
the search over possible transmissions. The resulting receivers nevertheless retain computations over the
full message alphabet. The next objective is to make decoding feasible as message length and activity grow,
allowing an explicit loss of accuracy where it buys useful computational savings. We pursue this through
nonlearned, algebraically motivated approximations, using the exact observations in Report 7 and the
approximation results in Report 9 as foundations. The fixed encoder and PUPE objective remain unchanged.

\section{Keep the target; identify the computation to remove}
Write $b=B_c$, $d=N_c$, $P=2^{B_p}$ and $M=P2^b=2^B$. With the balanced encoder of Report 8,
\begin{equation}
 \phi(p,v)=S_pD_p(-1)^{Lv},\qquad D_p=\diag(\sqrt{e_p}),\qquad
 y=\sum_{k=1}^K\phi(w_k)+z,\quad z\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_n).
 \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
The receiver knows the encoding rule, $K$ and $\sigma^2>0$. Messages and their patterns are unknown.
Every waveform has unit energy. IID uniform messages give the count posterior
\begin{equation}
 \pi_y(a)=\frac1{Z_y}\frac{K!}{M^K\prod_w a_w!}
  \exp\left[-\frac{\|y-\sum_w a_w\phi(w)\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right],\qquad \sum_w a_w=K.
 \label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
For an output count vector $c$ totaling $K$, the conditional PUPE is
\begin{equation}
 \risk_y(c)=1-\frac1K\sum_w\sum_{j=1}^{c_w}T_{w,j},\qquad
 T_{w,j}=\Prb(a_w\geq j\mid y).
 \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
The Bayes action repeatedly selects the largest available next tail. Approximations change how these
tails are obtained; a likely configuration or a small signal residual alone is a different decision rule.
Distinct-message sampling is a separate conditioned source, which still allows shared patterns.

There are three costs to separate. Payload processing should avoid enumerating $2^b$ words. The fixed
placement/energy bank already contains $Pd$ entries, so retaining it costs $\Theta(Pd)$. Finally, the
number of candidate configurations, samples and residual searches can grow with $K$. Removing one
exponential sum does not bound the others.

Current multiset-beam scoring costs roughly $O(KWnM)$ for width $W$; sparse columns can replace $n$ by
$d$ but leave $M$. The B12/B14 experiments retain $b=6,d=32,n=256$ and increase $P$ from 64 to 256.
They therefore identify useful inference mechanisms without testing longer polar payloads. A payload
scaling study must increase $b,d$ at a declared rate and also specify $P,n,K$ and noise/energy scaling.

\section{Use the exact conditional polar problem}
Given a hypothesized configuration of the other $K-1$ slots, form
$r=y-\sum_{j\ne k}\phi(w_j)$. Unit candidate energy gives
\begin{equation}
 f_r(p,v)=e^{s_p(v)},\qquad
 s_p(v)=\frac{r^T\phi(p,v)}{\sigma^2}
       =\sum_{i=1}^d h_{p,i}(-1)^{\ell_i^Tv},\qquad
 h_{p,i}=\frac{\sqrt{e_{p,i}}r_{r(p,i)}}{\sigma^2}.
 \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
Here $\ell_i^T$ is row $i$ of $L$. These are exact conditional weights, with polar LLRs $2h_{p,i}$.
The outside messages are receiver hypotheses, not known true transmissions.

\subsection{A polar list is a proposal, not a partition}
SCL can return a bounded list without enumerating payloads. It does not supply
$Z_p=\sum_v e^{s_p(v)}$ or an exact conditional sample. Its retained path metric is
\[
 m_p(v)=\sum_i\log(2\cosh h_{p,i})-s_p(v).
\]
The first term varies with the pattern. Cross-pattern comparisons must restore it or directly rescore
the physical waveforms. An empty CRC-valid list does not establish pattern absence.

For a deduplicated list $C(r)$ and declared guide scores $\widetilde s$, an available normalized proposal is
\begin{equation}
 q_C(w\mid r)=\frac{\ind\{w\in C(r)\}e^{\widetilde s(w)}}
                    {\sum_{u\in C(r)}e^{\widetilde s(u)}},\qquad
 q(w\mid r)=(1-\alpha)q_C(w\mid r)+\alpha/M,
 \label{eq:listproposal}
\end{equation}
where $0<\alpha<1$; use the uniform law if the list is empty. Random message bits supply the uniform
draw without an alphabet scan. A single-slot Metropolis--Hastings (MH) move accepts $u\to w$ with
\begin{equation}
 A(u,w\mid r)=\min\{1,e^{s_r(w)-s_r(u)}q(u\mid r)/q(w\mid r)\}.
 \label{eq:mh}
\end{equation}
The outside residual is identical in both directions. A sequential pair proposal needs its actual
reverse contexts as well. These constructions already exist in the earlier revisable-sampling prototype.
Full support ensures a valid target, but a missed word may still require order $M/\alpha$ draws to discover.

\subsection{Residual uncertainty changes the guide}
When $h$ users remain, including the next proposed word, Report 9's Gaussian continuation replaces the
unresolved sum by its mean and covariance. With one-message moments $\mu,\Sigma_x$, put
\[
 u=r-(h-1)\mu,\qquad \Sigma_h=\sigma^2I+(h-1)\Sigma_x,
 \qquad \widetilde s(x)=u^T\Sigma_h^{-1}x-\tfrac12x^T\Sigma_h^{-1}x.
\]
This is an approximation to the remaining-user convolution, not a change in physical noise.
For scalar $\Sigma_h$, the quadratic term is common and ordinary polar LLRs apply. A diagonal
covariance adds a pattern-dependent constant. A dense covariance introduces additional parity terms:
\begin{align}
 \widetilde s_p(v)={}&\sum_i a_i(S_p^T\Sigma_h^{-1}u)_i\chi_{\ell_i}(v)\nonumber\\
 &-\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}a_ia_j(S_p^T\Sigma_h^{-1}S_p)_{ij}\chi_{\ell_i+\ell_j}(v),
 \label{eq:quadraticcharacters}
\end{align}
where $a_i=\sqrt{e_{p,i}}$ and $\chi_g(v)=(-1)^{g^Tv}$. Thus a dense guide is generally not an ordinary
$d$-symbol SCL input. Dropping its extra terms is a further approximation. Exact physical weighting
can correct a guide's target bias, but cannot guarantee useful sampling or fast mixing.

\section{Build a normalized reference from independent parities}
\label{sec:envelope}
Report 9's character algebra gives another tractable inner operation. For a fixed pattern and affine
payload restriction $v=v_0+Rz$, $z\in\GF(2)^q$, group identical restricted rows:
\begin{equation}
 s(z)=H_0+\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}H_g\chi_g(z),\qquad g\ne0.
 \label{eq:characters}
\end{equation}
Grouping is exact. The physical conditional has at most $d$ groups; the dense Gaussian guide may have
$O(d^2)$. Choose an independent row set $F$, of rank $r_F$, and split the score into
\begin{align}
 s_F(z)&=H_0+\sum_{g\in F}H_g\chi_g(z),&
 D_F(z)&=\sum_{g\notin F}H_g\chi_g(z),\nonumber\\
 R_F&=\sum_{g\notin F}|H_g|,&
 Z_F&=2^{q-r_F}e^{H_0}\prod_{g\in F}2\cosh H_g.
 \label{eq:basis}
\end{align}
Independent parity assignments have equal multiplicity $2^{q-r_F}$. Consequently
$q_F(z)=e^{s_F(z)}/Z_F$ is normalized. Sample each retained parity independently with
$\Prb(g^Tz=0)=e^{H_g}/(2\cosh H_g)$, then solve the binary system and choose its free bits uniformly.
No payload enumeration or unknown normalizer is required.

Used alone, $q_F$ ignores the dependent terms. Alternatively it is a reference for exact correction.
Since $|D_F|\leq R_F$,
\begin{equation}
 L_F:=e^{-R_F}Z_F\leq Z:=\sum_z e^{s(z)}\leq U_F:=e^{R_F}Z_F,
 \qquad e^{s(z)}\leq U_Fq_F(z).
 \label{eq:partition}
\end{equation}
We call the last pointwise upper bound an \emph{envelope}. It bounds both a partition and the weight
of each possible word.

\subsection{Choose the basis and combine unknown patterns}
Greedy selection in decreasing $|H_g|$, keeping a row only if independent, minimizes $R_F$.
It also minimizes $U_F$ within this family: retaining a row reduces the log upper bound by
$|H_g|-\log\cosh H_g$, which increases with $|H_g|$. The independent-set exchange property gives
the same greedy ordering for either weight. This is optimality of this bound construction, not of the decoder.

For unconditioned patterns set $q=b$ and $U_{\rm all}=\sum_pU_{F,p}$. Propose a pattern and payload by
\begin{equation}
 Q(p,z)=\frac{U_{F,p}}{U_{\rm all}}q_{F,p}(z),\qquad
 a(p,z)=e^{D_{F,p}(z)-R_{F,p}}.
 \label{eq:rejection}
\end{equation}
Then $Qa=e^{s_p(z)}/U_{\rm all}$. Rejection sampling therefore returns an exact one-slot conditional,
with acceptance $Z_{\rm all}/U_{\rm all}$ and mean attempts $U_{\rm all}/Z_{\rm all}$.
The unknown partition controls runtime even though it is unnecessary to execute a draw.

The same bound certifies a list's conditional omitted mass. If its exact weight is $Z_C$, then
\begin{equation}
 \Prb(w\notin C\mid r)\leq1-Z_C/U_{\rm all}.
 \label{eq:listmass}
\end{equation}
This concerns one specified outside configuration. It does not certify that a multiuser search has found
the right configurations. For a distinct-message source, reject prohibited full words and use the allowed partition.

\subsection{A finite update need not wait for rejection to succeed}
Cap rejection at $L_a$ attempts and keep the current word on failure. For a fixed outside context this gives
\[
 \beta(r)\pi_r+(1-\beta(r))I,\qquad
 \beta(r)=1-(1-Z_{\rm all}/U_{\rm all})^{L_a},
\]
where $\pi_r$ is the exact conditional. This kernel is invariant because its success probability does not
depend on the current slot value. Using $Q$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mh} is another bounded-cost update.
Neither result establishes rapid movement of the full $K$-user chain.

\subsection{Dependent parities can make the envelope poor}
Suppose the score contains $g,h,g+h$ with coefficients $(a,a,-a)$, $a>0$. Writing
$x=\chi_g$, $u=\chi_h$ gives $s=a(x+u-xu)$: three assignments score $a$ and one scores $-3a$.
Any two-character reference has acceptance
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A(a)=\frac{3e^a+e^{-3a}}{e^a(2\cosh a)^2}\sim3e^{-2a}.
 \label{eq:conflict}
\end{equation}
The preferred signs conflict, so separate factor bounds cannot be attained together. The selected
$b=6,d=32$, CRC8 generator contains this triangle among its 28 distinct nonzero rows. Positive amplitudes
and distinct occupied resources allow a residual to realize these coefficients and zero others.
This is a realizable conditional obstruction, not a measurement of typical residuals.

\section{Refine selected states without discarding the rest}
\label{sec:repair}
A loose envelope need not be replaced everywhere. Suppose $E(w)=UQ(w)\geq f(w)>0$, with an available
normalized proposal $Q$. Evaluate exact weights on a finite set $A$ and define
\begin{equation}
 Z_A=\sum_{w\in A}f(w),\quad \tau_A=Q(A^c),\quad
 E_A(w)=\begin{cases}f(w),&w\in A,\\E(w),&w\notin A,\end{cases}
 \quad U_A=Z_A+U\tau_A.
 \label{eq:repaired-envelope}
\end{equation}
Then $f\leq E_A\leq E$ and $Z\leq U_A\leq U$. Exploring $w$ reduces the upper bound by
$E(w)-f(w)$. A useful state to refine is therefore one whose weight the reference overestimates;
it need not be a particularly likely transmitted message.

The unknown outside mass is at most $U\tau_A$, giving
\begin{equation}
 \Prb(w\notin A\mid r,p)\leq\frac{U\tau_A}{Z_A+U\tau_A}.
 \label{eq:repaired-miss}
\end{equation}
If a lower envelope $LQ\leq f$ is also available, the partition interval becomes
$Z_A+L\tau_A\leq Z\leq Z_A+U\tau_A$.

\subsection{The refined envelope is an explicit full-space proposal}
With probability $Z_A/U_A$, draw an explored word proportionally to its exact weight and accept.
Otherwise draw from $Q$ conditioned on $A^c$ and accept with probability $f(w)/E(w)$.
The proposal is $E_A/U_A$, its accepted density is $f/U_A$, and its acceptance is $Z/U_A$.
Mean attempts decrease after refinement, but the work of constructing $A$ must still be recovered.
Across patterns, select $p$ proportionally to its refined $U_{A,p}$.

The same proposal can be used without rejection for importance sampling. With $\pi=f/Z$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_w\frac{\pi(w)}{E_A(w)/U_A}\leq\frac{U_A}{Z},\qquad
 1+\chi^2(\pi\|E_A/U_A)\leq\frac{U_A}{Z}.
 \label{eq:secondmoment}
\end{equation}
The second inequality follows by bounding one factor of the squared density ratio and averaging the
other. Refinement improves this variance bound; it does not prove that its preparation is worthwhile.

\subsection{Sample outside an arbitrary list directly}
Repeatedly drawing $Q$ until it leaves $A$ would cost $1/\tau_A$ attempts. The parity reference avoids
this problem. An invertible binary coordinate change gives independent retained parities followed by
uniform free coordinates. Transform the explored words into these coordinates and build a prefix tree.

Let $m(\alpha)$ be the conditional probability of avoiding explored leaves below prefix $\alpha$.
Use $m=1$ where no explored descendant exists, $m=0$ at an explored leaf, and otherwise
\begin{equation}
 m(\alpha)=(1-p_i)m(\alpha0)+p_i m(\alpha1).
 \label{eq:complement}
\end{equation}
Here $p_i$ is the product law's probability of bit one. Choose the next bit $c$ with probability
$\Prb(X_i=c)m(\alpha c)/m(\alpha)$. The factors telescope to $Q(w)/\tau_A$ outside the list.
This is a direct conditional draw, including when the list holds almost all reference mass.

For $q$ free bits, the tree takes $O(|A|q)$ preparation and storage after coordinate conversion;
conversion can cost $O(|A|q^2)$. Traversal costs $O(q)$ plus the inverse map. The positive recursion
also avoids subtracting nearly equal masses. Very small probabilities still require stable arithmetic.

\section{Generate states by testing reliable parity coordinates}
\label{sec:flips}
The independent reference suggests a search rule as well as a sampler. Let $F$ span every nonzero
score character. Each retained parity disagrees with its favored sign with probability
\[
 p_j=\frac1{1+e^{2|H_j|}}.
\]
Enumerate assignments with at most $t$ disagreements, then evaluate their complete physical scores.
This is related to ordered-statistics decoding's reliable-basis reprocessing\cite{fossorier}.
The number of tested assignments is
\begin{equation}
 N_t=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(t,r_F)}\binom{r_F}{j}=O(r_F^t)\quad\text{for fixed }t.
 \label{eq:flips}
\end{equation}
It supplies a finite alternative to SCL discovery. Increasing the radius changes the polynomial degree.

If $r_F=q$, an assignment identifies one payload. Otherwise it identifies $2^{q-r_F}$ equally weighted
payloads, since all score characters lie in the span. Use that multiplicity in its mass and sample the
free bits uniformly. A parity cell cannot be treated as one full message in the outer count decoder.

The reference flip count is a sum of independent Bernoulli variables. A suffix dynamic program capped
at $t+1$ computes $\tau_t=\Prb_Q(\text{more than }t\text{ flips})$ in $O(r_F(t+1))$ work and storage.
The same tables sample exactly conditional on exceeding $t$. Thus the refined envelope
$U_t=Z_{A_t}+U\tau_t$ has both a computable total and an accessible complement sampler.
An SCL list can instead supply $A$, or be combined with this search after deduplication.

\subsection{One explored cell repairs the triangle}
For $s=a(x+u-xu)$, retain reference $ax+au$ and explore only its favored cell $(x,u)=(1,1)$.
Replacing that cell's envelope weight $e^{3a}$ by its exact weight $e^a$ gives
\begin{equation}
 U_0=3e^a+e^{-a},\qquad Z=3e^a+e^{-3a},\qquad Z/U_0\longrightarrow1.
 \label{eq:triangle-repair}
\end{equation}
The single-flip cells already attain their envelope weights, so exploring them does not improve this bound.
At $a=8$, acceptance changes from $3.38\times10^{-7}$ to $.999999962$.

The explored cell nevertheless holds only asymptotic target mass $1/3$. Its omitted-mass bound tends
to $2/3$. Restricting inference to that cell would be poor; the refined full-space proposal succeeds
because its direct complement sampler also visits the other modes. Improving a proposal and retaining
enough states for list-only inference are different objectives.

\subsection{Repeated conflicts require a different representation}
For $m$ independent triangles, target flip count tends to $\operatorname{Binomial}(m,2/3)$ as $a$ grows.
A fixed-radius list loses target mass as $m$ increases. More sharply, for fixed $m$ and $t<m-1$,
\[
 \tau_t\sim\binom{2m}{t+1}e^{-2a(t+1)},\qquad U\asymp e^{3ma},\qquad Z\asymp3^me^{ma},
\]
so refined acceptance still decays proportionally to $e^{-2a(m-t-1)}$. At $a=4$, radius-zero acceptance
is $.999888$ for one triangle but $3.82\times10^{-10}$ for four. Radius one only raises the latter to
$3.25\times10^{-7}$. A growing search radius can therefore recover exponential work.

These independent triangles have a cheaper exact solution: evaluate each four-state component separately.
In general, express characters in the retained basis and connect coordinates appearing in a common factor.
If component ranks are $r_i$, partitions and samples factor exactly, with enumeration costing roughly
$\sum_iD_i2^{r_i}$ after constructing the coordinate maps. Small joint groups can also replace parts of
a reference, while omitted factors retain explicit envelope bounds. More general elimination costs depend
on joint separator width, not the rank of each individual factor.

This is an exact control for the counterexample, not evidence of small components in the selected code.
With every nonzero generator character present, the checked $(b,d)=(6,32),(32,128),(88,512)$ constructions
(CRC8, CRC16 and CRC16 respectively) each have a single component of rank $b$.
Residual-specific zero coefficients, conditioning or an explicitly
bounded factor approximation may simplify them; useful small decompositions remain to be established.

\section{Reuse calculations across nearby residuals}
\label{sec:reuse}
Repeated proposal construction may dominate inference. For the same encoder, noise and allowed words,
compare residuals $r_0$ and $r=r_0+\Delta r$. Their exact score difference is
\[
 \Delta s(w)=\Delta r^T\phi(w)/\sigma^2.
\]
Choose bounds $l\leq\Delta s(w)\leq u$ and write $\Omega=u-l$. Unit energy gives the interval
$[-\|\Delta r\|/\sigma^2,\|\Delta r\|/\sigma^2]$ without searching words. Grouping the difference
score by pattern gives a possible refinement:
\[
 l=\min_p\left(c_{p,0}-\sum_{g\ne0}|c_{p,g}|\right),\qquad
 u=\max_p\left(c_{p,0}+\sum_{g\ne0}|c_{p,g}|\right).
\]
Intersect the two intervals. With cached row groups, the second calculation costs $O(Pd)$.

\subsection{Transfer conditional accuracy bounds}
Since $\pi_r\propto\pi_{r_0}e^{\Delta s}$,
\begin{equation}
 \TV(\pi_r,\pi_{r_0})\leq\tanh(\Omega/4).
 \label{eq:reuse-tv}
\end{equation}
For $\Omega=0$ the laws agree. Otherwise, let $X=e^{\Delta s}\in[a_-,a_+]$ and $m=\E X$. Convexity bounds the variation by
$(a_+-m)(m-a_-)/[(a_+-a_-)m]$. Maximizing at $m=\sqrt{a_-a_+}$ gives the displayed bound.
A cached approximate law with error $\eta_0$ has reused error at most
$\min\{1,\eta_0+\tanh(\Omega/4)\}$.

If a list has certified omitted mass at most $\varepsilon_0$ at $r_0$, then
\begin{equation}
 \pi_r(C^c)\leq\frac{e^\Omega\varepsilon_0}{1-\varepsilon_0+e^\Omega\varepsilon_0}.
 \label{eq:reuse-odds}
\end{equation}
Outside weights increase by at most $e^u$ while inside weights decrease by at most $e^l$; comparing
their odds proves the result. A desired tolerance $\varepsilon_\star>\varepsilon_0>0$ permits reuse if
\[
 \Omega\leq\log\frac{\varepsilon_\star(1-\varepsilon_0)}{\varepsilon_0(1-\varepsilon_\star)}.
\]
This can avoid another polar search. It certifies one conditional list, not complete multiuser coverage.

\subsection{Reuse a proposal with exact correction}
If a cached proposal satisfies $e^{s_{r_0}(w)}\leq U_0q_0(w)$, then
$e^{s_r(w)}\leq e^uU_0q_0(w)$. Draw from $q_0$ and accept with
$e^{s_r(w)}/(e^uU_0q_0(w))$. The new conditional is exact, and an available lower bound
$Z_0^-\leq Z_{r_0}$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A_r=Z_r/(e^uU_0)\geq e^{-\Omega}Z_0^-/U_0.
 \label{eq:reuse-acceptance}
\end{equation}
Thus the explored-state refinement and residual reuse combine through the same pointwise envelope.
Rejection can again be capped with a hold on failure, or $q_0$ can be used in MH.

For a fixed context, $C_0=U_0/Z_0^-$ gives $\pi_r/q_0\leq e^\Omega C_0$. The independence-MH transition
therefore dominates $e^{-\Omega}\pi_r/C_0$, giving contraction at most
$(1-e^{-\Omega}/C_0)^t$ after $t$ moves. Indeed its off-diagonal mass is
$\min\{q_0(w),\pi_r(w)q_0(v)/\pi_r(v)\}$ and each term has this lower bound.
The bound does not establish mixing of the joint chain whose outside users keep changing.

\subsection{Account for the cache itself}
A fixed bank of centers can be prepared from $y$. Select a center using the outside residual, so a
single-slot move uses the same proposal forward and backward. Pair moves require the corresponding
reverse choices. History-dependent adaptation needs a separate validity argument.

With $J_0$ centers and $J$ queries, charge $J_0$ constructions plus selection, comparison and physical
rescoring for all $J$ queries. Naive center selection costs $O(J_0n)$ per query; grouped comparison
adds $O(Pd)$. Low noise magnifies residual differences by $1/\sigma^2$, potentially requiring many centers.
These bounds identify when reuse is safe, not whether useful residuals admit a small cache.

\section{Discover a correlated pair without scanning payload pairs}
Single-slot proposals can miss configurations that require two simultaneous replacements. A bounded
pair search is therefore worth testing. Fix the other $K-2$ hypothesized slots and let $r$ be their
subtracted residual. First consider the hypothesis that both proposed messages use pattern $p$.
Report 7 already gives full-alphabet pair contractions and a noiseless same-pattern identifiability
criterion. The question here is different: can a finite noisy search propose useful pairs without
enumerating $2^{2b}$ payload pairs?

\subsection{Use a four-symbol polar channel}
Put $a_i=\sqrt{e_{p,i}}$ and $r_i=r_{r(p,i)}$. For coded bits $x,z\in\GF(2)$, the local channel weight is
\begin{equation}
 W_{p,i}(x,z)=\exp\!\left[-\frac{(r_i-a_i[(-1)^x+(-1)^z])^2}{2\sigma^2}\right].
 \label{eq:pairchannel}
\end{equation}
This is exact for the fixed outside context and same-pattern hypothesis. Because both users employ
the same binary polar transform, their paired input symbols form an additive $\GF(2)^2$ transform.
For two four-entry channel arrays $A,B$, its kernel $(u,v)\mapsto(u\oplus v,v)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 W^-(u)=\tfrac14\sum_{v\in\GF(2)^2}A(u\oplus v)B(v),\qquad
 W^+(v;u)\ \propto\ A(u\oplus v)B(v).
 \label{eq:pairkernel}
\end{equation}
Joint polar decoding on multiple-access channels is established; see
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.4255}{\c{S}a\c{s}o\u{g}lu, Telatar and Yeh} and
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.3123}{Nasser and Telatar}.
The proposed use here keeps the existing encoder, frozen positions and two CRC constraints.

A deterministic list search extends a path by four symbols at an information position and by $(0,0)$ at a jointly
frozen position, retains at most $L_2$ paths, and checks both CRCs. Its principal arithmetic can be
$O(16L_2d\log d)$, with $O(4L_2d)$ working memory using a standard shared-path implementation;
sorting all extensions adds $O(4L_2d\log(4L_2))$. Re-encode and physically rescore surviving pairs.
This last step also restores the unoccupied-coordinate likelihood terms needed to compare patterns.

The local kernel identities are exact; a finite SCL list is an approximation. Its usual recursion
marginalizes free future symbols, without fully imposing future frozen/CRC constraints, and pruning
can discard the useful pair. It yields neither the constrained pair partition nor an exact posterior
draw. The existing frozen schedule was not designed here as a new two-user MAC code, so the cited
coding theorems do not establish its finite-list performance.

\subsection{A cheaper XOR guide has a missing code factor}
There is also a route through two ordinary binary polar searches. Write the ordered payload pair as
$(v,v\oplus\delta)$, and define
\[
 m_i(\delta)=\ind\{(L\delta)_i=0\},\qquad
 \theta_i=2a_ir_i/\sigma^2,\qquad \kappa_i=2a_i^2/\sigma^2.
\]
The signals cancel where $m_i=0$ and add where $m_i=1$. After removing the common factor
$e^{-\|r\|^2/(2\sigma^2)}$, the exact pair weight is
\begin{equation}
 f_p(v,\delta)=\exp\!\left[\sum_i m_i(\delta)
              \{\theta_i\chi_{\ell_i}(v)-\kappa_i\}\right].
 \label{eq:pairxorweight}
\end{equation}
For fixed $\delta$, this is an ordinary polar likelihood in $v$, with LLRs $2m_i(\delta)\theta_i$.
Under the IID uniform source conditioned on $p$, $v$ and $\delta$ are independent uniform payloads;
$\delta=0$ correctly represents repeated messages.

Marginalizing each sign independently suggests XOR-code LLRs
$\gamma_i=\log\cosh\theta_i-\kappa_i$. But the resulting weight $G_p$ omits code consistency:
\begin{align}
 G_p(\delta)&=\exp\!\left[\sum_i m_i(\delta)\gamma_i\right],\qquad
 \sum_v f_p(v,\delta)=2^bG_p(\delta)\mathcal C_p(\delta),\label{eq:xorcorrection}\\
 \mathcal C_p(\delta)&=2^{-b}\sum_v\prod_{i:m_i(\delta)=1}
       \frac{e^{\theta_i\chi_{\ell_i}(v)}}{\cosh\theta_i}.\nonumber
\end{align}
$\mathcal C_p=1$ when all active rows are distinct, nonzero and linearly independent. Constant, repeated or
dependent rows generally prevent that simplification. $\mathcal C_p$ is a likelihood correction, not a
probability. On one tiny $b=2,d=8$, CRC4 fixture, the normalized guide probabilities for
$\delta=00,01,10,11$ are $(.508,.399,.058,.035)$, whereas the exact probabilities are
$(.203,.765,.014,.019)$: the preferred difference reverses.

One bounded candidate procedure decodes a list of $L_\delta$ differences using $\gamma$, then a
conditional list of $L_v$ payloads for each difference, and physically rescores the resulting pairs.
Ignoring list sorting, its per-pattern search cost is
$O((L_\delta+L_\delta L_v)d\log d)$. The first list can lose a useful difference before the exact
score is evaluated. The earlier character envelopes can instead bound the masked partition in
\eqref{eq:xorcorrection}, but constructing that bound costs an inner operation for each tested
difference. Neither route is selected as the winner. Report 7's existing rank condition for
noiseless unordered-pair identification does not resolve this noisy discovery problem.

\subsection{Normalize the finite proposal and retain the count target}
For a fixed residual, pool and deduplicate the proposed \emph{ordered} full-message pairs in $C(r)$. With
$g_r(u_1,u_2)=\exp[-\|r-\phi(u_1)-\phi(u_2)\|^2/(2\sigma^2)]$, define
\begin{equation}
 q_C(\mathbf u\mid r)=\frac{\ind\{\mathbf u\in C(r)\}g_r(\mathbf u)}
                              {\sum_{\mathbf z\in C(r)}g_r(\mathbf z)},\qquad
 q(\mathbf u\mid r)=(1-\alpha)q_C(\mathbf u\mid r)+\alpha/M^2.
 \label{eq:pairproposal}
\end{equation}
Use the uniform pair law if the list is empty. Its draw requires only random message bits, and its
normalization requires only the finite list. With fixed or uniformly selected slot indices, the IID
ordered-slot prior cancels, and the block MH acceptance is
\begin{equation}
 \min\!\left\{1,\frac{g_r(\mathbf u)}{g_r(\mathbf w)}
                  \frac{q(\mathbf w\mid r)}{q(\mathbf u\mid r)}\right\}.
 \label{eq:pairmh}
\end{equation}
The outside residual is identical in both directions. A stochastic factorized same-pattern proposal
instead has density $q_P(p\mid r)q_\delta(v\oplus u\mid p,r)q_v(v\mid v\oplus u,p,r)$ for
the ordered pair $((p,v),(p,u))$; its actual forward and reverse densities must be evaluated.

If an unordered pair list is used, divide each pair's proposal mass equally among its two orderings,
or its single ordering for a repeated message. There is no additional factorial factor in the
ordered-slot update: projecting all $K$ slots to counts already produces the IID count prior.
Choosing slot pairs according to their current messages requires the corresponding reverse selection
probability. Distinct-message sampling requires its own exclusions and proposal normalization.

For $J_2$ residual contexts and $R_p$ tested patterns per context, charge the pair-search cost
$J_2R_p$ times, physical rescoring, and any $O(Pd)$ pattern screening in every context. The bank still
costs $\Theta(Pd)$ memory; a retained pool contains up to $R_pL_2$ ordered pairs in addition to the
per-pattern working memory. A naive scan of possible slot pairs adds $O(K^2)$ choices.
For an IID prior pair, the probability of a common pattern is only $1/P$. With different patterns,
overlaps can couple different polar symbol indices, so \eqref{eq:pairkernel} is generally no longer
an aligned memoryless channel. This proposal therefore addresses a restricted correlated move;
full support alone gives no useful discovery or mixing bound for the remaining configurations.
Count-tail estimation and the PUPE readout are still required after these moves.

\textbf{Finite verification and next boundary.} A separate identity script checks all 16 payload pairs
in each of 20 tiny residual fixtures, the four-symbol kernel, and full-support pair MH detailed balance
on 64 ordered states. It implements no joint SCL decoder and supplies no performance result.
A useful next experiment is a bounded conditional comparison of joint four-symbol and XOR-first
candidate recall, cost, and CRC-valid list size at growing $b,d$, followed by tests in estimated
multiuser residuals. Those measurements would decide whether this operation merits an end-to-end bridge.

\section{Keep the final count calculation sparse}
For $H$ weighted legal configurations $a^{(h)}$ with normalized weights $\rho_h$, their measure has tails
\[
 \widehat T_{w,j}=\sum_h\rho_h\ind\{a_w^{(h)}\geq j\}.
\]
Accumulate a histogram of positive counts for each observed word and take reverse cumulative sums.
Since $\sum_w\max_h a_w^{(h)}\leq\sum_{h,w}a_w^{(h)}=HK$, storage for nonzero tail levels is $O(HK)$,
not $O(MK)$. A heap of next tails allocates the output in $O(C_s+K\log C_s)$ work, where $C_s\leq HK$
is the number of observed words. Labels, waveform storage and inference remain separate costs.

For a retained multiset beam, weights are proportional to the physical likelihood divided by
$\prod_w a_w!$, normalized over unique retained multisets. This is the posterior conditioned on the beam.
Ordered-slot samples instead use prior $M^{-K}$; adding another factorial factor would count the source twice.

\subsection{Restricting outputs differs from restricting explanations}
For a candidate set $C$, define
\[
 \mu_{\rm out}=\frac1K\E\sum_{w\notin C}a_w,\qquad
 \varepsilon_C=\Prb\left(\sum_{w\notin C}a_w>0\mid y\right),
\]
where the expectation is under the full posterior. Every $C$-supported action has risk at least
$\mu_{\rm out}$. If its tails are still computed under the full posterior, the best such action has
\emph{excess} risk at most $\mu_{\rm out}$: remove outside entries from a global optimal action and refill
inside. Uniform retained-tail error $\eta$ adds at most $2\eta$. Refilling requires nonempty $C$ for IID
counts, or $|C|\geq K$ for distinct counts.

Conditioning the entire posterior on all words belonging to $C$ gives a different bound. Its Bayes action
has excess risk at most $\varepsilon_C$, because the full law is a mixture of the conditional law and
its complement. Conditional inference regret $\delta_C$ gives
$(1-\varepsilon_C)\delta_C+\varepsilon_C$.

For example, assign probability $1/4$ to $a^4$ and $3/4$ to $b^3c$, with $C=\{a,b\}$.
The outside-copy fraction is $3/16$, but conditioning selects $a^4$, with excess risk $1/2$.
The full-posterior constrained action is $b^3a$. Their risks are $.75$ and $.375$, versus Bayes risk $.25$.
This decision-law example shows why candidate recall alone cannot justify conditioning the posterior.

\subsection{Representation can be small even when obtaining it is hard}
If $H$ independent exact posterior samples were available, Bernoulli concentration and a union bound give
\begin{equation}
 \Prb\left(\max_{w,j}|\widehat T_{w,j}-T_{w,j}|>\eta\right)\leq2MK e^{-2H\eta^2}.
 \label{eq:uniformtails}
\end{equation}
Thus $H\geq[B\log2+\log(2K/\delta)]/(2\eta^2)$ suffices for excess risk at most $2\eta$ with probability
$1-\delta$. Unseen words have empirical tail zero, so sparse readout suffices despite the all-word bound.
Only $\log M$ enters this sample count. Producing the independent samples is the unresolved step;
correlated chain states or weighted importance draws do not satisfy this premise automatically.

\section{Use overlap parities to separate a mixed-pattern block}
\label{sec:parity-cover}
The same-pattern channel does not handle general mixed-pattern overlaps. Report 7 supplies their
exact parity factorization, but its pair operator still groups and copies all $2^b$ payload weights.
Combining those parities with affine one-user envelopes gives another finite operation. Its cost is
exponential in a chosen interaction rank; a small rank is a condition to test, not an assumption.

\subsection{Condition one endpoint of every retained interaction}
Fix $m$ slot indices, their hypothesized ordered patterns $p_1,\ldots,p_m$, and the residual $r$ after
subtracting the other slots. Removing the common residual norm and $m$ unit self-energies leaves
\begin{equation}
 S(v_1,\ldots,v_m)=\sum_k s_k(v_k)
 -\sum_{k<l}\sum_{g,h}J_{kl}(g,h)\chi_g(v_k)\chi_h(v_l),\qquad
 s_k(v)=r^T\phi(p_k,v)/\sigma^2,
 \label{eq:cover-score}
\end{equation}
where $J_{kl}(g,h)$ sums $\sqrt{e_{p_k,i}e_{p_l,j}}/\sigma^2$ over shared resources whose local
generator rows are $g,h$. Terms with a zero row are constants or unaries; absorb them first.
This can reduce the interaction rank without approximation.

For slot $k$, choose a character subspace $W_k$ with independent row basis $H_k$, rank $r_k$.
Call a remaining factor \emph{covered} when $g\in W_k$ or $h\in W_l$. Condition
$\alpha_k=H_kv_k$. For $g\in W_k$, its sign is now a known function of $\alpha_k$.
A covered factor therefore becomes a constant if both endpoints are fixed, or shifts the unary
coefficient at its unfixed endpoint. Keeping all covered factors gives exactly
\begin{align}
 S_W&=c(\alpha)+\sum_k t_{k,\alpha}(v_k),\qquad H_kv_k=\alpha_k,\nonumber\\
 Z_W&=\sum_\alpha e^{c(\alpha)}\prod_{k=1}^m Z_{k,\alpha},\qquad
 Z_{k,\alpha}=\sum_{v:H_kv=\alpha_k}e^{t_{k,\alpha}(v)}.
 \label{eq:cover-partition}
\end{align}
There are $2^R$ sectors, $R=\sum_kr_k$. Every payload tuple belongs to exactly one, so no extra
$2^{-R}$ factor appears. Write each affine support as $v=v_0+Nz$ and use the restricted character
algebra. It retains all polar and CRC constraints; ordinary unconstrained SCL does not automatically
enforce these additional parity conditions.

For a pair, let $W_p$ span all nonconstant left overlap rows and set $W_q=\{0\}$. Conditional on the
left overlap signs, the right law is an ordinary polar likelihood with shifted LLRs; the left law
is affine-restricted. This needs $2^{r_p}$ sectors, versus $2^{r_p+r_q}$ entries in the two-sided
overlap table, and avoids its full-payload grouping stage. Reversing the orientation gives another
choice. Neither sector's polar partition is free.

For several users, form each slot's \emph{joint} row span across its assigned interactions.
Rows shared by different neighbours are charged once. A three-user star control below has two
rank-two edge interfaces at the center but only rank two jointly, hence four sectors. Independent
edge summaries would fail to enforce that they came from the same central payload.

\subsection{Limit rank, bound the omitted factors, or correct exactly}
With a rank budget, some factors may remain uncovered. Set
\[
 D=S-S_W,\qquad T(W)=\sum_{\text{uncovered }(k,l,g,h)}|J_{kl}(g,h)|.
\]
Then $|D|\leq T$, giving
\begin{equation}
 e^{-T}Z_W\leq Z\leq e^TZ_W,\qquad
 \TV(\pi,\pi_W)\leq\tanh(T/2).
 \label{eq:cover-error}
\end{equation}
The variation bound uses log-weight oscillation at most $2T$. It bounds every conditional count-tail
error. For this fixed outside configuration and pattern tuple, a Bayes action under $\pi_W$ has
conditional PUPE regret at most $2(m/K)\tanh(T/2)$: only $m$ true copies can change, so each action's
loss varies by at most $m/K$. This does not bound the stationary error of approximate block updates
whose contexts keep changing.

Exact correction need not evaluate the inner partitions. For every sector, construct available
pointwise envelopes $e^{t_{k,\alpha}}\leq U_{k,\alpha}q_{k,\alpha}$ and lower partition bounds
$L_{k,\alpha}$ using Section~\ref{sec:envelope} or its refinements. Define
\begin{equation}
 U_W=\sum_\alpha e^{c(\alpha)}\prod_kU_{k,\alpha},\quad
 L_W=\sum_\alpha e^{c(\alpha)}\prod_kL_{k,\alpha},\quad
 Q(\mathbf v)=\frac{e^{c(\alpha)}\prod_kU_{k,\alpha}}{U_W}
                         \prod_kq_{k,\alpha}(v_k).
 \label{eq:cover-proposal}
\end{equation}
Sample a sector with the displayed finite weights, then sample its independent affine payload laws.
The sector is determined by the resulting tuple, so its density is evaluable. The full physical
weight satisfies $e^S\leq e^TU_WQ$. Accept with $e^S/(e^TU_WQ)$ to obtain an exact fixed-pattern
block draw, with
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A=Z/(e^TU_W)\geq e^{-2T}L_W/U_W.
 \label{eq:cover-accept}
\end{equation}
Alternatively use this full-support proposal in a valid block MH update. The IID prior is uniform
on ordered slots, including repeated messages; count factorials must not be added again. Distinct
sampling introduces further exclusion factors requiring separate treatment.

\subsection{Choose and pay for the representation}
An executable rank-limited heuristic starts with empty spans, adds an endpoint row that captures the
largest total uncovered coefficient magnitude after taking its span, and stops at budget $R$.
It is not the earlier optimal independent-basis selection. Even one-sided selection can fail:
give keys $e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2$ weight 9 each and keys $e_3,e_4$ weight 10 each. With rank budget two,
greedy captures 20; the first two coordinates capture 27. General cover selection includes graph
vertex-cover choices when every slot has one bit. Small families of declared candidate covers can
therefore be compared without claiming a globally optimal choice.
Naively testing all endpoint rows and rescanning factors after each addition costs
$O(RE^2b^2)$ binary/scalar work for $E$ factors; the complete one-sided pair cover avoids that search.

Let $E$ count grouped cross-factors and $C_{k,\alpha}$ be the cost of constructing a restricted unary
envelope. After geometry and binary-linear preprocessing, setup costs
\[
 O\!\left(2^R\left[E+\sum_kC_{k,\alpha}\right]\right)
\]
with the worst sector cost understood. Independent-basis envelopes have polynomial construction
cost in $d,b$, but their acceptance can be exponentially small. Store $2^R$ sector weights and
rebuild the selected sector, or cache its inner proposals at additional memory cost. Rebuilding pays
the selected inner construction cost on every attempt. Charge physical
rescoring and all repeated attempts; the certified mean-attempt bound is $e^{2T}U_W/L_W$.
A larger cover can simplify inner laws enough to compensate for its extra sectors, so minimum rank
alone is not the runtime objective.

For $J$ contexts and $H$ tested pattern tuples per context, pay these costs $JH$ times unless a
specific reuse bound applies. Exhaustive pattern search costs $P^m$ tuples; conditioning on one
tuple does not discover it. Geometry may be cached, but residual-dependent unary coefficients change.
The pattern bank still costs $\Theta(Pd)$, and complete inference still needs count-tail readout.
For independent uniform $d$-subsets of $n$ resources, expected pair overlap is $d^2/n$; increasing
payload length does not ensure small overlap rank. Rank $b$ still gives $2^b$ sectors in this
one-sided procedure. Low noise also amplifies $T$ by $1/\sigma^2$.

\subsection{Evidence and relationship to earlier work}
Conditioning variables to simplify inference is established; see
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.2740}{Bidyuk and Dechter, \emph{Cutset Sampling for Bayesian Networks}}.
Report 10's DEC-09 already admits conditioned feature cutsets. The concrete extension here combines
Report 7's physical overlap characters, Report 9's affine inner algebra and Report 11's pointwise
envelopes to avoid an explicit payload table in a fixed-pattern block operation. This targeted
comparison does not establish research novelty.

The independent script \path{tests/polar_tractability_structural_checks.py} checks all 512 payload
tuples in each of 12 tiny three-user fixtures, four cover choices, a same-pattern duplicate control,
partition bounds, normalized proposal correction and the greedy counterexample. Maximum discrepancy
is $5.33\times10^{-15}$. In the first fixture the exact rank-two cover uses four sectors; its
envelope acceptance is $.350$, illustrating that exact separation still leaves inner sampling cost.

A placement/generator census for $B=12,b=6,d=32,n=256$, seed 3201, gives 31,941 one-sided sectors
over all 2,016 distinct unordered pattern pairs, versus 1,093,347 two-sided nonconstant table entries.
Constant-row interactions were absorbed into unaries in both counts. These are representation counts,
not a runtime or large-payload result: sectors require inner calculations. A measured rank/acceptance/
construction-cost comparison on actual residuals is the next useful boundary.

\section{Turn proposal bounds into expected decoder loss}
\label{sec:expected-decoder}
The preceding guarantees have different scopes. Envelope slack bounds conditional rejection cost and
importance variance. Omitted mass, residual oscillation and omitted interactions bound changes in a
specified posterior or conditional decision. None alone controls a finite joint chain's distance from
the full posterior. A high acceptance rate, or an empirical importance ESS, is not such a guarantee.
We now combine the parity envelope, multiset search and independent sampling to obtain a complete
decoder with an expected-loss bound.

\subsection{Bound the whole configuration with one shared word law}
For any fixed $t\in\mathbb R^n$, construct the earlier normalized parity proposal so that
\[
 e^{t^\top\phi(w)}\leq U_tq_t(w)\qquad\text{for every message }w.
\]
For ordered $W=(w_1,\ldots,w_K)$, let $S(W)=\sum_k\phi(w_k)$ and
$f_y(W)=M^{-K}e^{-\|y-S(W)\|^2/(2\sigma^2)}$. Completing the square gives
\begin{align}
 -\frac{\|y-S\|^2}{2\sigma^2}-t^\top S
 &=-t^\top y+\frac{\sigma^2\|t\|^2}{2}
   -\frac{\|S-(y-\sigma^2t)\|^2}{2\sigma^2},\nonumber\\
 f_y(W)&\leq C_tQ_t(W),\qquad Q_t(W)=\prod_{k=1}^Kq_t(w_k),\label{eq:tuple-envelope}\\
 C_t&=\left(\frac{U_t}{M}\right)^K
       \exp\left[-t^\top y+\frac{\sigma^2\|t\|^2}{2}\right].\nonumber
\end{align}
The product is a proposal, not an independence assumption about the posterior. The final negative
square accounts for every user interaction. The bound is valid for any fixed $t$; the present
isotropic choice $t=y/[\sigma^2+(K-1)/n]$ is only a guide, not an optimized tilt.
For $K=1$ this choice gives $y-\sigma^2t=0$ and $\|S\|^2=1$. Retaining that known square
tightens $C_t$ by the factor $e^{-1/(2\sigma^2)}$; the implementation uses this specialization.

Let $A$ be a finite beam of complete multisets. Its exact unnormalized count weights are
$f_\#(a)=o(a)f_y(W_a)$, where $o(a)=K!/\prod_w a_w!$.
Then $Z_A=\sum_{a\in A}f_\#(a)\leq Z_y$. Drawing a multiset with probability $f_\#(a)/Z_A$ and
uniformly shuffling its slots defines an ordered proposal $q_A(W)=f_\#(a)/(Z_Ao(a))$ on that beam.
Use the full-support mixture
\begin{equation}
 Q_\lambda(W)=(1-\lambda)Q_t(W)+\lambda q_A(W),\qquad 0\leq\lambda<1.
 \label{eq:exchangeable-mixture}
\end{equation}
Both components are exchangeable. A tuple's density needs only its product probability and multiset
orbit factor; no enumeration of $K!$ orderings is required. Arbitrary slot-specific products would
lose this simplification when symmetrized.

Freeze the beam, tilt and proposal before drawing $H$ independent tuples. Their physical importance
weights and normalized density ratios obey
\begin{equation}
 \omega(W)=\frac{f_y(W)}{Q_\lambda(W)}
 \leq C=\frac{C_t}{1-\lambda},\qquad
 r(W)=\frac{\omega(W)}{Z_y}\leq \bar\kappa=\frac{C}{Z_A},\qquad
 \E_{Q_\lambda}r=1.
 \label{eq:global-ratio}
\end{equation}
This supplies a computable bound without evaluating the full partition. A common product can still
assign negligible mass to useful multiuser configurations, and the envelope can be loose. The beam
component helps represent correlated explanations, but neither useful variance nor a useful
$\bar\kappa$ is automatic.

\subsection{Compare decisions before normalizing their tails}
For a count-tail indicator $I_{w,j}(W)=\ind\{a_w(W)\geq j\}$, define
$\widetilde T_{w,j}=H^{-1}\sum_h r(W_h)I_{w,j}(W_h)$.
It is unbiased for $T_{w,j}$. Its empirical normalized version divides every tail by the same
positive number $H^{-1}\sum_h r(W_h)$, so both versions select the same count action $\widehat c$.
If $c^*$ is the exact Bayes action, direct comparison of their total tail rewards gives
\[
 0\leq \risk_y(\widehat c)-\risk_y(c^*)
 \leq 2\max_{w,j}|\widetilde T_{w,j}-T_{w,j}|.
\]
There is therefore no additional denominator-error term for this decision comparison.

Each $rI_{w,j}$ lies in $[0,\bar\kappa]$ and has variance at most $\bar\kappa$.
The Bernstein exponential-moment inequality for both signs, followed by a maximum over the $MK$ tails,
gives, with $\ell=\log(2MK)=B\log2+\log(2K)$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad
 \E\!\left[\risk_y(\widehat c)-\risk_y(c^*)\mid y,A,t\right]
 \leq \min\left\{1,\;
 2\sqrt{\frac{2\bar\kappa\ell}{H}}+\frac{2\bar\kappa\ell}{3H}\right\}.
 \quad}
 \label{eq:expected-regret}
\end{equation}
Specifically, the centered sample means have exponential-moment bound
$\log\E e^{s(\widetilde T-T)}
\leq (\bar\kappa/H)s^2/[2(1-\bar\kappa s/(3H))]$.
Applying this to the $2MK$ signed errors and optimizing $s$ bounds their expected maximum by
$\sqrt{2\bar\kappa\ell/H}+\bar\kappa\ell/(3H)$.
The expectation is over independent proposal draws conditional on the frozen construction.
It is not a guarantee for every realized output, and correlated MH samples cannot be substituted for $H$.
Unvisited words have empirical tail zero, so the calculation remains sparse despite the all-word proof.

\subsection{What can be predicted, and what still requires measurements?}
Averaging \eqref{eq:expected-regret} over observations and any independent pilot randomness gives an
ensemble excess-PUPE bound. Absolute expected PUPE is the Bayes risk plus that excess:
\[
 \E[\mathrm{PUPE}]=\E_Y[\risk_Y(c_Y^*)]
                  +\E_{Y,\mathrm{draws}}[\risk_Y(\widehat c)-\risk_Y(c_Y^*)].
\]
Thus even a useful approximation-error bound does not determine the fixed encoder's irreducible error.
Evaluating the Bayes term, or obtaining tight typical envelope ratios, may itself be difficult.
The bound in \eqref{eq:expected-regret} may simply equal one.
This occurs throughout the current multiuser smoke, despite much smaller measured conditional regret.
It is therefore a valid diagnostic but not yet a useful numerical multiuser performance prediction.

Bounds are useful for rejecting unsafe simplifications, transferring a conditional guarantee,
and exposing which quantity must improve. Repair monotonically improves a fixed envelope bound;
it need not improve runtime at a fixed budget. A smaller upper bound also does not prove that one
decoder outperforms another. Compare matched PUPE and cost empirically, while retaining these
diagnostics to explain failures. The coding bounds plotted in the benchmark concern different codes
or ensembles; their gap is not an estimate of this encoder's recoverable decoder loss.

\section{Use geometric aliases without discarding count uncertainty}
\label{sec:affine-alias-update}
Report 7's first four-user ambiguity now has a complete finite coset classification. More generally,
for a payload subspace $U$, put
$W_U=\operatorname{span}(\mathcal G\cap U^\perp)^\perp$.
All cosets of $U$ inside $a+W_U$ have exactly the same same-pattern waveform.
Report 8 owns their physical distance and energy-design consequences. The receiver question here is
whether this equality can be used without constructing the $2^b$ payload table.

\subsection{An exact conditional move found from the current messages}
Maintain the existing ordered $K$-slot representation under the IID uniform source. Choose four slots
on one hypothesized pattern. If their payloads are distinct and have XOR zero, they form an affine
plane $a+U$: two independent differences give its basis. Otherwise leave the state unchanged.
Compute a binary basis of $W_U$, draw a uniform $d\in W_U$ using independent basis coefficients,
and replace all four selected payloads $v_i$ by $v_i\oplus d$.

\textbf{Invariance.} Translation leaves $U$ and hence $W_U$ unchanged. The reverse shift is the same
$d$, with the same probability. The block waveform is identical by the Report-7 criterion, so the
full likelihood, including every outside pattern and any observation noise, is identical. The IID
ordered-slot prior is uniform. Acceptance is therefore one. Conditional on this ordered translation
orbit, the draw is an exact uniform heat-bath update; it is not a draw from the entire four-slot
conditional posterior. Uniform selection among all slot quadruples is valid. Selection among
same-pattern quadruples is also valid if it depends only on pattern occupancies, which this move preserves.
Payload-dependent selection requires its reverse probability.

The distinct-message source can use the same symmetric proposal and reject shifts intersecting any
outside message. This gives an invariant kernel, not an exact conditional draw on the legal part of
the orbit. With a nonuniform source the prior ratio must enter acceptance. In count space, uniform
coset replacement alone is generally wrong: outside duplicates change $1/\prod_w a_w!$.
For example, outside counts $c_{12}=2,c_1=1$ give prior factors $1/6$ and $1/2$ for the two
four-user aliases in Report 7. Ordered-slot selection supplies the compensating factor of three
automatically. It must not be discarded when projecting the operation to counts.

\subsection{Preserve the tails analytically, or sample full identities}
Let $c$ be the outside counts, $A=a+W_U$ and $J=2^{\dim W_U-2}$.
Each translated plane contains four distinct payloads; every payload in $A$ belongs to exactly one
of its $J$ cosets. On this IID ordered-slot orbit, for a word on the chosen pattern,
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(c_v+\mathbf1\{v\text{ in the block}\}\geq j\mid\text{orbit})
 =\mathbf1\{c_v\geq j\}
  +\frac{\mathbf1\{c_v=j-1\}\mathbf1\{v\in A\}}{J}.
 \label{eq:affine-orbit-tail}
\end{equation}
Outside-pattern counts remain fixed. This identity keeps each message's multiplicity information;
one arbitrary representative plane would not. Membership in $A$ uses binary linear equations,
not a payload scan. For a bounded list of queried words this is a direct Rao--Blackwell operation.
Alternatively, draw the shift and retain the four explicit full-message IDs in the ordinary sparse
count-tail accumulator. That avoids constructing the affine set, while retaining sampling uncertainty.

These compact tail functions do not automatically solve the final allocation over a mixture of many
overlapping affine sets. Discovering the maximum aggregate score can require additional intersection
work. The sampled-ID route uses the existing finite-chain or importance framework and its actual
error account; orbital invariance alone supplies no finite-sample confidence or mixing bound.

\subsection{Complete cost and why this is a local repair}
The generator can be cached once by encoding $b$ basis payloads; charge those $b$ polar/CRC operations
and $N_cb$ bits of storage. For a supplied plane, row restriction, rank and nullspace construction cost
conservatively $O(N_cb^2+b^3)$ bit operations with $O(N_cb+b^2)$ storage. A shift costs $O(b^2)$ bit
operations and four ID updates; no likelihood normalization or rejection loop is needed for IID.
An independent check can re-encode the four words. Grouping slots by pattern costs $O(K)$; searching
all eligible quadruples instead of selecting one costs $\sum_p\binom{K_p}{4}$ plane tests.
For $T$ visited contexts these preparation costs recur unless the identical $U$ is cached explicitly.
$S$ recorded full configurations still cost $O(SK)$ sparse count updates and the Report-10 readout.

The retained finite script checks the physical orbit, analytic tails, factorial-prior warning and a
100-bit nullspace calculation with no alphabet allocation. It does not implement a new DEC family.
The method cannot leave its waveform class, discover an unrelated missing mode or remove irreducible
encoder uncertainty. In the current bank, Report 7 bounds the prior probability of the recorded
four-user witness by only $6.8\,10^{-5}$ at B12/$K=26$. This is therefore an exact targeted repair,
not a promising explanation of a large PUPE gain by itself. Near-alias moves require physical rescoring;
Report 9 gives a conditional likelihood-variation bound for merging them.

\textbf{Prior work.} Symmetry-based orbital MCMC is established
(\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5396}{Niepert, 2012}). The specialization here obtains an exact
local translation group from binary row restrictions, with explicit slot/count accounting and tail queries.
It does not inherit a general rapid-mixing guarantee or establish a new general MCMC principle.

\subsection{Sparse Fourier discovery requires an actual measurement design}
There is also a direct connection to sparse Walsh recovery. If the encoder supplies every character
$H^Tt+d$, $t\in\GF(2)^q$, for a rank-$q$ binary map $H$, then
\begin{equation}
 2^{-q}\sum_t\chi_t(j)\sum_v a_v\chi_{H^Tt+d}(v)
 =\sum_{v:Hv=j}a_v\chi_d(v).
 \label{eq:character-bin}
\end{equation}
This follows by character orthogonality. The $d=0$ packet gives nonnegative bin counts.
Additional offsets completing the rows of $H$ to a basis identify a bin containing just one distinct
payload, even if its multiplicity exceeds one: all offset magnitudes equal its count exactly when
all supported payloads have the same basis bits. This is a noiseless singleton test, not a noisy rule.

For $C$ hash maps, $D=b-q+1$ such packets per map require $CD2^q$ physical symbol observations
before sharing identical rows, $O(CDq2^q)$ short-transform work, plus row construction, singleton
processing, association and peeling. Constructing explicit row keys costs $O(CD2^q b)$ bit operations
after $O(Cb^3)$ basis completion; procedural generation can instead retain $O(Cb^2)$ map bits.
With a queue and each bin decoded at most once, at most $K$ recovered distinct payloads incur
$O(KCb^2)$ additional conservative bit work for labels, hashing and updates. Memory includes
$CD2^q$ observations/bin values, map data and $O(Kb)$ output bits, with count and numerical precision
specified separately. Repeated residual contexts multiply this work; searching all patterns adds $P$
conditional problems unless a discovery rule avoids that scan. Known integer counts and full payload
labels must survive peeling.
Worst-case supports can have no singleton. Under uniform energy over these observations, division by
the symbol amplitude and the normalized short transform give bin-noise variance
$\sigma^2N_c/2^q$; reusing observations also creates inter-packet dependence. More queries at fixed
unit power therefore do not come with unchanged noise precision.

The affine-query and peeling principle is established in
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06336}{Li et al., SPRIGHT, 2015}, whose analysis uses designed
queries and stated random-support/noise assumptions. Repeated IID messages change the nonzero-coefficient
law. The fixed generator need not contain these packets, and an unknown mixed-pattern observation
is not direct access to one payload's Walsh
coefficients. Adding the packets changes the signature length, energy distribution and receiver.
The identity identifies a concrete design interface; it does not establish scalable count-PUPE recovery
for the reference. A next useful control is packet availability and physical bin precision before
implementing a complete peeling receiver.

\section{Combine the operations and charge the complete cost}
Let $J$ count residual contexts, $R_p$ patterns decoded per context, $L_s$ the SCL width, $C$ pooled words,
$W$ beam width and $H$ complete proposals. An efficient SCL implementation costs $O(L_sd\log d)$ time
and $O(L_sd)$ space\cite{tal}; prototype copying, sorting and CRC work require their own accounting.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{.25\textwidth}YY@{}}\toprule
Operation & Principal work & Remaining question\\\midrule
Polar lists then multiset beam & $J[Pd+P\log P+R_pL_sd\log d]$; then $KWCd$ plus bookkeeping & Do the lists and retained configurations cover useful explanations?\\
Parity reference & Per pattern/context: $Dq^2+q^3+D\log D$; draw: $q^2+Dq$ & Is its envelope tight enough to justify construction?\\
Radius-$t$ refinement & $N_tDr_F$ scores and $r_F(t+1)$ suffix tables & Is a small radius effective on the residuals encountered?\\
Arbitrary-list refinement & Coordinate conversion, exact scores and $|A|q$ tree work & Does reduced envelope slack repay the preparation cost?\\
Residual reuse & Center selection, comparison and new physical scores & Can enough contexts share a small bank?\\
Joint same-pattern list & $16J_2R_pL_2d\log d$ plus sorting, screening and scores & Is joint discovery useful often enough to repay its cost?\\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Here $D$ is the number of grouped characters. Bounds use elementary arithmetic; binary packing and cached
maps can improve implementation costs. All-pattern preparation multiplies by $P$. Persistent encoder storage
is $O(Pd+dq)$, cached basis maps can add $O(Pq^2)$, and dense residual storage costs $O(n)$ per retained context.
Dense Gaussian guidance additionally requires covariance work, up to $O(n^3)$ factorization and $O(n^2)$ memory.

Fresh discovery at every beam parent gives $J$ of order $KW$; sequential importance sampling can give $J=HK$.
Sampling $H$ ordered tuples from their actual proposal $q(W\mid y)$ uses weights
\[
 \omega(W)=\frac{M^{-K}\exp[-\|y-\sum_k\phi(w_k)\|^2/(2\sigma^2)]}{q(W\mid y)}.
\]
A whole-tuple prior mixture bounds these unnormalized weights by $1/\alpha$; separate stagewise mixtures
only give $\alpha^{-K}$. Neither controls evidence-relative variance by itself. Similarly, good one-slot
rejection acceptance does not guarantee global mixing. Shared lists, small exact factor groups, selective
refinement and residual reuse can combine, but their joint construction and discovery costs must be counted.
The shared product in \eqref{eq:exchangeable-mixture} instead prepares one all-pattern reference,
then uses $HK$ word draws/density evaluations and $H$ physical scores. It avoids $HK$ fresh reference
builds, at the cost of a less conditional guide. Beam setup, orbit bookkeeping and waveform caching
remain additional costs. Mixture importance sampling is established methodology\cite{owen}; the
encoder-specific envelope and count-decision bound are the constructions examined here.

\section{Finite decoder implementations}
The following IID-source variants now use procedural waveforms and sparse count tails. All know $K$
and the physical noise variance. They are complete finite-budget receivers, not exact posterior solvers.
Their numbering continues Report 10's bank and the DEC-14--24 combination study.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lYY@{}}\toprule
ID & Discovery or proposal & Inference and count decision\\\midrule
DEC-25 & Screen patterns; ordinary polar SCL lists & Bounded multiset beam; exact posterior restricted to retained states\\
DEC-26 & Screen patterns; reliable-basis flip candidates & Same multiset beam and restricted count tails\\
DEC-27 & Independent-parity envelope across all patterns & Physically corrected single-slot MH; empirical count tails\\
DEC-28 & Replace selected envelope weights by exact weights & Same MH kernel with direct complement sampling\\
DEC-29 & Freeze a small bank of repaired proposals & Select by outside residual; exact physical MH correction\\
DEC-30 & Four-symbol same-pattern joint polar lists & Alternate pair-list MH and single-slot SCL-list MH\\
DEC-31 & XOR-first same-pattern pair lists & Same alternating kernels; exact retained-pair scoring\\
DEC-32 & Single-slot polar list mixed with the uniform prior & Corrected single-slot MH; empirical count tails\\
DEC-33 & Common tilted parity product mixed with a multiset beam & Independent physical importance weights; expected-loss bound \eqref{eq:expected-regret}\\
DEC-34 & Union of SCL and reliable-basis candidates & Multiset beam; candidate containment at a fixed context only\\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The beams merge identical partial multisets. Their continuation score uses the isotropic residual guide;
at the final depth, each retained multiset has weight proportional to
$[K!/\prod_w a_w!]\exp[-\|y-\Phi a\|^2/(2\sigma^2)]$.
The chains start from a declared bounded beam, update ordered slots, and add no further factorial term.
Their default remains SCL; the matched Job037 comparison uses the same width-32 basis initializer for
all correction variants. Its top configuration need not equal the beam's count-tail Bayes output.
Pair moves have full support through their uniform ordered-pair mixture; ordinary single-slot moves also
change patterns. Same-pattern list proposals therefore do not impose a same-pattern posterior restriction.
Each finite chain has initialization and sampling error; invariant kernels alone do not bound that error.
DEC-33 freezes its proposal before independent draws and records the expectation bound separately from
per-output certificates. DEC-34's candidate union need not give nested final beams: pruning changes
later residual contexts, so adding candidates does not guarantee lower PUPE.

Refinement explores a capped set of actual payloads; the exclusion tree retains every other payload.
A cache is fixed after initialization, and its selected proposal depends only on the outside residual,
which is identical forward and reverse for that block. Each proposal's actual normalized density enters
MH. No stale likelihood or uncorrected adaptive chain is substituted. An empty CRC-valid discovery list
remains empty: list proposals then use the prior, while a beam parent receives one declared prior draw.
The latter prevents an empty output but supplies no candidate-coverage guarantee.

The procedural interface stores integer message IDs, on-demand waveforms, retained configurations and
only visited-word tail entries. It has no $M$-length array. The implementation currently caches a
dense $n$-vector per generated waveform, costing $O(nC_{\rm generated})$ within a call; budgets still
control this cost. Parity proposals rebuild all $P$ pattern envelopes unless cached. Pair recursion uses
explicit array copies, so an optimized SCL complexity claim would be premature.
The common small-$B$ harness retains its explicit encoder and converts sparse hard counts to its legacy
array format. That harness is not itself a large-$B$ execution path.

Independent tests recover REF tails from exhaustive tiny beams, verify repaired draw laws and
single/pair MH balance, and match procedural outputs to the legacy adapter. A separate bounded
$B=100$ test uses $P=4,b=98,d=128,n=256$, CRC16 and integer IDs beyond 64 bits. All variants execute
for $K=1,2$ without an alphabet table; the noiseless single-user observation is recovered at declared
positive variance. This is an execution/control test, not evidence of useful multiuser PUPE at $B=100$.
The new mixed-pattern parity-cover operation in Section~\ref{sec:parity-cover} remains a checked algebraic
construction; it is not silently included in DEC-25--34. Further tiny exhaustive checks verify
DEC-33's ordered mixture, repeated-word orbit factors, global envelope and beam evidence lower bound.

\textbf{Locate the information loss.} Let $\mathcal C$ be the visited-word union and let
$c_w=\max\{j:\widehat T_{w,j}>0\}$ for a saved numerical tail table, with empty maximum zero.
Any action confined to these capacities satisfies
\[
 \ell(a,\widehat a)\geq 1-\frac1K\sum_w\min(a_w,c_w).
\]
This follows directly from $\widehat a_w\leq c_w$; it is an optimistic capacity bound, because the
capacities need not occur together in one retained configuration. It is evaluated using truth and
is not a receiver certificate. Numerical zeros may arise from state omission, finite sampling or underflow.
For DEC-26 at $B=14,K=26,4$dB, the visited-word missing-copy bound is $.04412$, the positive-tail capacity
bound $.24439$, and actual loss $.31510$. At 10dB they are $.00336,.02639,.04778$.
Word discovery alone therefore understates the remaining joint-state/tail problem. This diagnostic
does not yet separate pruning from numerical concentration or finite-chain effects.

The correction DEC-32 accepts on average 114.37 of 128 proposals at $B=14,K=26,10$dB, but changes its state
only $.582$ times. Self-proposals explain why nominal acceptance is not a mixing diagnostic.
For DEC-33 at $B=12,K=26,4/10$dB, all 768 decisions per cell equal DEC-26's decisions, despite ESS about 512
and more than five times its recorded cost; its expected-risk bound remains 1. These data close neither
importance sampling in general nor the unreturned $B=14$ runs. They do deprioritize this fixed mixture.
Likewise SCL-only DEC-25, the fixed cache DEC-29 and union-beam DEC-34 are not priorities for another broad sweep at
these budgets. XOR-pair DEC-31 remains an ablation against simpler DEC-32; B14 variants DEC-27/28 and four-symbol DEC-30
remain unresolved pending completed runs.

\textbf{Next discriminating test.} Keep DEC-14 as the explicit accuracy reference and DEC-26 followed by DEC-32 as
the procedural reference. On a declared set of physical frames, record where true prefixes disappear
from the beam, and vary screening breadth separately from state width. Truth is used for diagnosis,
never for proposal selection. Then compare a small correction budget at matched elapsed work, retaining
all normalization, repeated contexts and count readout. A mixed-pattern affine provider is worthwhile
only if coordinated replacement is the measured obstruction and its setup/conditional draw cost is
competitive. More decoder families are not required before this test.

\section{Established results and remaining questions}
Normalized proposals, finite-set repair, residual reuse and sparse count decisions avoid payload
enumeration in declared operations. Overlap conditioning extends these to mixed patterns, but interaction
rank and inner acceptance can still be costly. The counterexamples rule out automatic sufficiency of
independent factors, fixed flip radii or small lists.

After the joint-state diagnostic, compare useful corrections from matched initial configurations and
increase payload length at declared $P,n,d,K$ scaling. Measure parity-cover rank, setup and acceptance
on actual residuals. The present identities and returned small-payload results establish no fixed-PUPE
large-payload scaling result.

\textbf{Relation to existing methods.} Reliable-basis reprocessing and polar list decoding are
established\cite{fossorier,tal}, as are combinations of search, bounding and importance sampling, and
partition refinement for rejection sampling\cite{lou,kuck}. Approximate codebook probability has also
been used for polar reliability and soft outputs\cite{yuan}. The present operations specialize these
principles to the balanced ODMA posterior and count decision. A research contribution must identify a
specific new construction or guarantee, or a useful measured improvement in complete-receiver cost and PUPE.

\textbf{Verification.} The four earlier algebra scripts check 40 character fixtures, 30 refinement
fixtures, 40 balanced residual pairs and 20 same-pattern pair fixtures, including the stated failure
controls, with discrepancies below $10^{-11}$. The mixed-pattern checks and independent implementation
tests are described above. Commands, finite budgets and local smoke outcomes are indexed in the experiment
bank and research log. These double-precision checks are not interval certificates or evidence of global mixing.
