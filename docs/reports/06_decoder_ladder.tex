\section{Scope and common model}

After matched-filter combination across receive antennas, all five learned decoders use
\[
 y=Ca^\star+z,
 \qquad C=[c_1,\ldots,c_M]\in\C^{n\times M},
 \qquad a^\star\in\Z_+^M,
 \qquad \one^Ta^\star=K.
\]
Here $K$ is known to every current decoder. The intended constrained codebooks have unit-energy columns but need not be
orthogonal. D0--D2 retain the full $M=2^B$ message axis. D3 and D4 instead receive a bounded candidate list and generate
only its columns. The cost of producing that list is outside those decoders and must be reported separately.

Conditioned on $K$ uniform transmissions, the joint count vector is multinomial and one marginal is
\[
 A_m\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(K,1/M).
\]
For $K/M\ll1$, collisions are negligible and this reduces to a known-cardinality Bernoulli support model. The Poisson
law with rate $K/M$ is another discrete approximation to the same marginal, but it allows counts above $K$ and discards
the fixed-total dependence. D2--D4 therefore use Bernoulli inference; the local section decoder in Report 4 retains its
exact Binomial count denoiser. A Poisson denoiser remains appropriate when the population size itself is random.

The decoder progression has two branches:
\begin{align*}
 \mathrm{D0}&\xrightarrow{\text{global learned context}}\mathrm{D1},\\
 \mathrm{D0}&\xrightarrow{\text{analytic effective channel}}\mathrm{D2}
 \xrightarrow{\text{bounded candidates}}\mathrm{D3}
 \xrightarrow{\text{learned geometry}}\mathrm{D4}.
\end{align*}
D1 asks how much an expressive full-message correction can improve performance. D2--D4 instead separate the analytic,
scalable, and expressive changes so that their effects can be measured.

\section{Common data-consistency step}

Let $a^t$ be the soft estimate at layer $t$. Every decoder preserves the physical forward and adjoint operations
\[
 r^t=y-Ca^t,
 \qquad g^t=\Re\{C^Hr^t\}.
\]
Thus gradients pass through the actual transmitted codewords. D0 and D1 form the learned gradient step
$u^t=a^t+\eta_tg^t$; D2--D4 use a calibrated sufficient statistic derived below. Hard nonnegative integer projection is
used only for evaluation.

\section{D0: learned scalar calibration}

D0 treats every coordinate as the same scalar channel. It forms one variance proxy per received sample by blending
residual energy with the known physical noise variance, then uses
\[
 \ell_{j}^{t}
 =\operatorname{clip}_{[-30,30]}\!\left(
 \gamma_t\frac{u_j^t-1/2}{\tau_t}
 +\beta_t\log\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}+b_t\right),
 \qquad \rho=K/M.
\]
The proposal is $p_j^t=\sigma(\ell_j^t)$, rescaled so that its total mass is $K$, then damped with the previous state.
Each layer learns step size, residual/noise mixture, variance scale, evidence gain, prior gain, bias, and damping.

D0 is cheap and supplies the baseline shared by all later decoders. Its limitations are also precise: one scalar
variance cannot represent codeword-dependent interference; prior gain, evidence gain and temperature are not uniquely
identified; and multiplying all probabilities to obtain mass $K$ is not a Bernoulli posterior projection.

\section{D1: expressive global correction}

D1 keeps D0 unchanged up to its base logit. A feature vector for message $j$ contains $u_j^t,a_j^t,g_j^t$, the base
logit, residual/noise statistics, and factor occupancy. A learned analysis map produces a latent $h_j^t$. Fixed-number
attention slots pool latent context within messages sharing a factor pattern, within messages sharing a local value,
and across the whole message set. A learned correction is then added:
\[
 \ell_j^{t,\mathrm{D1}}=\ell_j^{t,\mathrm{D0}}+\Delta_\theta(h_j^t,\text{pooled context}).
\]
The final correction head is initialised to zero, so an untrained D1 equals D0 exactly. Its fixed-slot implementation is
linear rather than quadratic in $M$, but it still stores and processes all $M$ message states. D1 is therefore an
expressive small-$B$ benchmark, not the scalable receiver. Its correction can improve ranking, but its logits need not
remain calibrated likelihood ratios.

\section{D2: analytic effective-channel calibration}

D2 returns to D0 and replaces its scalar variance heuristic. It starts from the prior mean $a_j^0=\rho$, rather than
D0's zero state, so the initial residual error is mean zero under the Bernoulli approximation. Define the real decision matrix
\[
 \bar C=C\quad\text{for real $C$},
 \qquad
 \bar C=\begin{bmatrix}\Re C\\ \Im C\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{for complex $C$},
 \qquad G=\bar C^T\bar C=\Re\{C^HC\}.
\]
With $d_j=G_{jj}$, one adjoint coordinate is exactly
\[
 g_j^t=d_j(a_j^\star-a_j^t)
 +\sum_{k\ne j}G_{jk}(a_k^\star-a_k^t)+\bar c_j^T\bar z.
\]
Adding back the known self-term gives the decision statistic
\[
 \zeta_j^t=g_j^t+d_ja_j^t=d_ja_j^\star+\xi_j^t,
 \qquad
 \xi_j^t=\sum_{k\ne j}G_{jk}(a_k^\star-a_k^t)+\bar c_j^T\bar z.
\]
Its inactive and active means are $0$ and $d_j$. Under a diagonal, conditionally uncorrelated error approximation, its
variance is
\[
 \omega_{j,t}^{\rm ana}=\nu d_j+\sum_{k\ne j}G_{jk}^2v_k^t,
 \qquad
 \nu=\begin{cases}\sigma^2,&\text{real noise},\\ \sigma^2/2,&\text{circular complex noise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Here $v_k^t=a_k^t(1-a_k^t)$. Equal variances alone do not make this exact: fixed-$K$ projection correlates coordinates,
and reusing the same observation across layers introduces dependencies unless an extrinsic or Onsager correction is
available.

The implementation avoids both the full $n\times M$ codebook and the $M\times M$ Gram matrix. Selected columns are
visited in bounded chunks to accumulate a small real frame. Let
\[
 s_j=\sum_kG_{jk}^2
 =\bar c_j^T(\bar C\bar C^T)\bar c_j,
 \qquad
 \bar v_{-j}^t=\frac{1}{M-1}\sum_{k\ne j}v_k^t.
\]
The implemented mean-field approximation is
\[
 \widehat\omega_{j,t}=\alpha_t\left[\nu d_j+\bar v_{-j}^t(s_j-d_j^2)\right],
 \qquad \alpha_t>0,
\]
where $\alpha_t$ absorbs model mismatch. Under the equal-variance Gaussian channel, the likelihood ratio is
\[
 e_{j,t}=\log\frac{p(\zeta_j^t\given A_j=1)}{p(\zeta_j^t\given A_j=0)}
 =\frac{d_j(\zeta_j^t-d_j/2)}{\widehat\omega_{j,t}}.
\]
D2 can equivalently be derived from $u_j^t=a_j^t+\eta g_j^t$: both the separation of its two means and its variance
scale by $\eta$ and $\eta^2$, so $\eta$ cancels exactly. D2 therefore has no learned step size and requires no spectral
norm estimate. Its only learned quantities per layer are $\alpha_t$ and damping.

D2 adds the known prior only after computing this evidence. Rather than multiplying probabilities by a common factor,
it finds the unique scalar $\lambda_t$ satisfying
\[
 p_j^t=\sigma\!\left(e_{j,t}+\log\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}+\lambda_t\right),
 \qquad \sum_jp_j^t=K.
\]
This is a product-Bernoulli projection onto the known expected cardinality, not the exact joint fixed-$K$ posterior. It
keeps every $p_j^t$ in $[0,1]$.
D2--D4 do not clip their evidence logits. For a uniform prior, the constant $\log(\rho/(1-\rho))$ is absorbed by
$\lambda_t$; its substantive roles are to initialise $a^0$ and to make the extension to nonuniform extrinsic priors
explicit.
D2 remains a global $M$-state reference. Its analytic geometry is cached for a frozen encoder; joint encoder learning
recomputes the two chunked $O(n^2M)$ frame passes. Its current construction is not a $B=100$ decoder.

\section{D3: candidate-restricted D2}

Let a proposer return $K<Q\ll2^B$ payloads
\[
 \mathcal C_b=\{w_{b1},\ldots,w_{bQ}\},
 \qquad
 C_b=[\phi(w_{b1}),\ldots,\phi(w_{bQ})]\in\C^{n\times Q}.
\]
D3 applies the complete D2 recurrence with $C_b$ and a $Q$-length state. Its current implementation forms the bounded
$Q\times Q$ candidate Gram once per forward pass; this is independent of $2^B$ but must still be counted. The compact
hash/amplitude generator from Report 5 constructs each selected column on demand. D3 itself therefore creates neither
an $n\times2^B$ codebook nor a $2^B$ state. The complete path is $M$-free only with the direct procedural generator; the
small-$B$ comparison adapter still stores global row and label lookups. Without a calibrated extrinsic proposer prior,
D3 uses the neutral conditional prior $K/Q$. Proposal scores derived from the same observation cannot simply be added
as independent evidence without double counting.

The restricted physical model is
\[
 y=C_ba_b^\star+\epsilon_b,
 \qquad
 \epsilon_b=z+\sum_{w\notin\mathcal C_b}a_w^\star\phi(w).
\]
If every active payload is proposed, $\epsilon_b=z$ and D3 isolates the effect of replacing the global axis by a bounded
candidate axis. If an active payload is absent, no D3 update can recover it; known-cardinality projection can then force
mass onto a false candidate. Every non-oracle evaluation must therefore report proposal recall and $Q$ before PUPE.

Exhaustive small-$B$ candidates certify equivalence with D2. Oracle-completed lists give a labelled upper bound on
candidate-conditioned recovery. Neither is evidence of a scalable proposer. D3 becomes a complete scalable receiver
only when paired with a measured, sub-exhaustive, high-recall proposal algorithm.

\section{D4: learned candidate-geometry correction}

D4 adds expressivity only after D3 has removed the global message axis. Its node features contain the D3 state,
decision statistic, channel evidence, effective variance, column energy, candidate density and optional proposal score.
The gradient is omitted because $g_j^t=\zeta_j^t-d_ja_j^t$ is redundant. Every candidate pair has the normalised Gram coefficient
\[
 \widetilde G_{ij}=\frac{c_i^Hc_j}{\|c_i\|_2\|c_j\|_2}.
\]
For the generated hash family, edge features may additionally contain the fraction of common hash bins and whether the
two messages share an amplitude-bank label. With node embedding $h_i^t$ and edge feature $e_{ij}$, the implemented
permutation-equivariant correction has the form
\[
 \gamma_{ij}^t=\tanh f_\theta(e_{ij}),
 \qquad
 m_i^t=\frac{\sum_{j\ne i}\gamma_{ij}^tV_\theta h_j^t}
 {\max\{1,\sum_{j\ne i}|\gamma_{ij}^t|\}},
\]
\[
 \widetilde\Delta_{i,t}=o_\theta\!\left(h_i^t+g_\theta(h_i^t,m_i^t,\bar h^t)\right),
 \qquad
 \Delta_{i,t}=\widetilde\Delta_{i,t}-\frac1Q\sum_{j=1}^Q\widetilde\Delta_{j,t},
 \qquad
 e_{i,t}^{\rm D4}=e_{i,t}^{\rm D3}+\Delta_{i,t},
\]
where $\bar h^t$ is global candidate context. The output head $o_\theta$ is bias-free and zero-initialised, so D4
initially equals D3 exactly. Centering removes the candidate-common logit direction that known-$K$ projection cannot
identify; without it, BCE could reward a shift that changes neither probabilities nor decisions. Cardinality projection
and damping then proceed as in D2.

D4 can learn effects missed by the mean-field variance, including signs and interactions of particular correlations.
It can also overfit to candidate-list composition, destroy evidence calibration, or spend $O(Q^2)$ work learning an
effect already captured analytically. D4 is therefore the performance-oriented candidate benchmark; D3 is its causal
control.

\section{Training objective}

D0 and D1 historically use class-balanced support BCE with a Smooth-L1 count auxiliary. D2 and D3 train their analytic
pre-prior channel evidence; D4 trains the corrected pre-prior discriminant, which need not remain a calibrated LLR:
\[
 \mathcal L_{\rm ev}
 =\frac12\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{j\in S}\operatorname{BCE}(e_j,1)
 +\frac12\frac{1}{|S^c|}\sum_{j\notin S}\operatorname{BCE}(e_j,0),
\]
with weighted deep supervision over layers. This prevents the learned variance from cancelling the rare-event prior.
Let $K_{\mathcal C}=\sum_ja_j^\star$ be the target mass on the represented axis. The count auxiliary is
\[
 \mathcal L_{\rm count}
 =\frac1{\max(K_{\mathcal C},1)}\sum_j\operatorname{SmoothL1}(a_j,a_j^\star),
 \qquad
 \mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm ev}+0.1\mathcal L_{\rm count}.
\]
For global or recall-one candidate targets, $K_{\mathcal C}=K$. If the list misses a transmission, D3/D4 still
project to mass $K$ while the target contains less mass; this deliberate failure diagnostic is not a suitable training
target. Current candidate-learning checks therefore use oracle-complete lists.
There is no activity $\ell_1$ penalty. Under the known-$K$ constraint, $a_j\ge0$ and $\one^Ta=K$ already imply
$\|a\|_1=K$, so such a penalty would be constant.

\section{Complexity and experimental meaning}

Let $\mathcal A(P)$ denote one forward/adjoint pair for $P$ retained columns, $d$ a latent width, $S$ a fixed attention
slot count, and $T$ the number of unrolled layers.
\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{0.92}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lYYY@{}}
\toprule
Decoder & State and cost & Learned part & Purpose \\
\midrule
D0 & $M$; $O(T[\mathcal A(M)+M])$ & Layer scalars & Scalar PGD baseline \\
D1 & $M$; D0 $+O[T(Md^2+MSd)]$ & Global correction & Value of full-axis context \\
D2 & $M$; D0-like; fixed setup $O(n^2M)$ & Scale and damping & Analytic calibration \\
D3 & $Q$; $O(nQ^2+T[\mathcal A(Q)+Q])$ & D2 scalars & Bounded refinement \\
D4 & $Q$; D3 $+O[T(Q^2d+Qd^2)]$ & Graph correction & Learned residual geometry \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Comparisons should pair encoder, channel samples, layer count and stopping rule. D2 should have D0-like layer cost after
geometry setup, fewer parameters and cleaner calibration, but D1 has much greater small-$B$ capacity. D3 should equal
D2 with exhaustive candidates and approach it as recall tends to one. Any trained D4 gain must justify its runtime and
calibration cost relative to its exactly equal initial D3 state.

D4 ablations should change one mechanism at a time: exact $\sum_{k\ne j}G_{jk}^2v_k^t$ variance; full covariance or
Onsager/extrinsic correction; top-$k$ Gram edges; Gram-only versus hash/label features; local LMMSE preconditioning; or
shared recurrent weights.

\evidence{D0/D1 are established small-$B$ baselines; D2--D4 are an incremental test ladder. Local checks certify
implementation, not improved performance. Candidate-conditioned decoding is not scalable complete-message recovery
until its proposer is non-oracle and measured.}
